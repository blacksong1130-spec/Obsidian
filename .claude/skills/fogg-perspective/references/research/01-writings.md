## 1. The Fogg Behavior Model (B=MAP)
Fogg's most foundational contribution, evolving from B=MAT (Motivation, Ability, Trigger) to B=MAP (Motivation, Ability, Prompt).
- **Behavior (B):** Occurs when three elements converge simultaneously.
- **Motivation (M):** The desire to do the behavior. Fogg notes this is the most "fickle" and unreliable component.
- **Ability (A):** The capacity to perform the behavior. Fogg’s primary innovation is that **Ability is the most effective lever**. If you make a behavior "Tiny" (extremely easy), you don't need high motivation.
- **Prompt (P):** The trigger that tells you to "do it now." No behavior happens without a prompt.

## 2. The 7 Steps of Behavior Design
A systematic process for designing any behavior change, not just habits:
1. **Clarify the Aspiration:** Defining the high-level goal (e.g., "Reduce stress").
2. **Explore Behavior Options:** The "Swarm of Behaviors"—brainstorming all possible actions.
3. **Match with Specific Behaviors:** Finding "Golden Behaviors" (High impact + High motivation to do).
4. **Start Tiny:** Shrink the behavior to the "ridiculously easy" level (e.g., "one pushup").
5. **Find a Good Prompt:** Using the "Anchor" technique (After [Existing Routine], I will [New Tiny Habit]).
6. **Celebrate Successes:** Creating the feeling of "Shine" to wire in the habit.
7. **Troubleshoot, Iterate, & Expand:** Refining the design if it fails.

## 3. Key Theoretical Shifts
- **Captology (Computers As Persuasive Technologies):** His 2003 work focused on how interactive computing can change attitudes and behaviors.
- **The Information-Action Fallacy:** Fogg asserts that "Information alone does not change behavior." He critiques the "Education Model" of change, arguing that design, not knowledge, is the driver.
- **Behavior Wizard:** A taxonomy of 15 types of behavior change (Green, Blue, Purple, Gray, Black paths crossed with Dot, Span, Path durations).

## 4. Primary vs. Interpreted
- **Fogg Says:** Habits are "wired" by emotion (specifically the feeling of success/Shine), not by repetition over 21 or 66 days.
- **Interpretations:** Often focus on the "Tiny" aspect (starting small) but overlook the **"Prompt" (Anchor)** and **"Celebration" (Shine)** components, which Fogg considers equally essential.
