## 1. The Pivot: From Persuasive Tech to Behavior Design (2011)
- **The Decision:** In 2011, Fogg renamed the "Persuasive Technology Lab" to the "Behavior Design Lab."
- **Reasoning:** He wanted to distance his work from "persuasion" (which implies manipulation) and focus on "design" (which implies empowerment). 
- **Refinement:** He shifted from "How can tech change people?" to "How can humans use psychology to change themselves?"

## 2. Ethical Reflections
- **Frustration with Silicon Valley:** Fogg has expressed personal frustration that his "Facebook Class" (2007) is often remembered for its viral growth hacks rather than his teachings on ethics.
- **The "Two Maxims":** He codified his ethical stance into two rules:
    1. Help people do what they *already want* to do.
    2. Help people feel successful.
- **Decision to focus on Health:** Post-2011, his lab prioritized "Lifestyle Medicine," focusing on diabetes prevention, mental health, and wellness rather than commercial engagement.

## 3. Institutional Move (2021)
- **Stanford Medicine:** He moved his lab into Stanford Medicine’s Division of Health and Human Performance. 
- **Goal:** To apply behavior design to chronic disease management and human flourishing, further distancing himself from the "app engagement" industry.

## 4. Choice of "Tiny" as a Strategy
- Fogg decided to focus on "Tiny" not just as a tool, but as a **philosophy of self-compassion**. This was a conscious move away from "Hard Work" or "Grind" culture, which he views as scientifically unsound for long-term change.
