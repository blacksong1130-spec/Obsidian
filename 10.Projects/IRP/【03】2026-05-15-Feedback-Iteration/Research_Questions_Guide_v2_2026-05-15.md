# Research Questions Guide — All Phases
**Why Eating Well Feels Like Nothing: Redesigning the Feedback Loop for Dietary Behaviour**
*Version 2 · Updated 2026-05-15*

---

## Overview

| Phase | Format | Duration | Participants |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Individual space visit + shared meal + semi-structured conversation | ~90 min | 3 (individual) |
| Phase 2 | One-day photo diary (self-administered) | Per meal, 10–15 min | Same 3 from Phase 1 |
| Phase 3 | Group lunch session + facilitated discussion | ~60 min | 4–5 (group) |
| Phase 4 | Design probe interview | ~40 min | Phase 1/2 participants (individual) |

---

## Phase 1 — Spatial Observation, Shared Meal & Semi-Structured Conversation

**Total: ~90 minutes**

---

### Part A: Space Observation (~15 minutes)
*Before the meal. Researcher observes and takes handwritten notes. Questions are asked conversationally — do not read them as a list.*

**Opening prompt:**
> "Before we eat, could you show me around the space where you usually eat?"

**For participants who mainly cook at home:**

- "Could you show me your kitchen / the place where you prepare food?"
- "Would you mind opening the fridge and showing me how things are arranged in there?"
- "Where do you keep snacks? What about vegetables and fruit?"
- "What's the first thing you usually see when you open the fridge?"
- "When you're not really thinking, where do you usually reach for something to eat?"
- "Which foods here are ready to eat right now? Which ones need some effort before you'd eat them?"

**For participants who mainly eat takeaway:**

- "Where did you eat your last takeaway meal? Could we go there?"
- "What's the space usually like around you when you eat?"
- "What's usually on the table or surface where you eat?"
- "Where does your phone usually sit when you're eating?"
- "Is this a typical setup for you, or is today a bit different?"

*Researcher notes (written, not said aloud): what food is visible vs. hidden; what the 'default grab' is; how food is arranged; what the eating environment looks and feels like.*

---

### Part B: Shared Meal + Semi-Structured Conversation (~75 minutes)
*Eat together first. Transition naturally into conversation — no need to separate these into distinct segments.*

**Opening (before audio recording begins):**
> "Thanks for having me today. I just want to chat about your everyday experience of eating — there are no right or wrong answers, and nothing to do with weight or calories. Would it be alright if I recorded our conversation? Only I will listen to it, and everything will be anonymised in the report."

*(Start recording only after consent is confirmed.)*

---

**Warm-up: Daily eating patterns (~10 minutes)**

- "Can you walk me through what a typical day of eating looks like for you? Which parts are planned, and which just happen?"
- "Where do you usually eat? What's around you when you eat?"
- "Has the way you eat changed much in the last year or two? What changed?"

---

**Failure moments: Past attempts to change eating habits (~15 minutes)**

- "Think of a time when you genuinely tried to eat differently and it didn't stick. What made it hard — too much effort, felt meaningless, something else?"
- "When do you find yourself eating without really thinking about it? Is it a particular place, mood, or time of day that triggers it?"
- "If you could change one thing about your eating environment — not through willpower, just by changing the space itself — to make eating well a bit easier, what would it be?"

---

**Body signals: How it feels after eating (~15 minutes)**

- "When a meal feels right — like it really worked for you — how do you know? What does your body feel like about ten minutes after?"
- "Have you ever finished a meal and felt unsure whether you'd eaten well? What does that uncertainty feel like?"
- "Can you remember a specific moment when you clearly felt 'that's enough'? What brought on that feeling?"

---

**Tracking tools (~10 minutes)**

- "Have you ever used an app or any other tool to keep track of what you eat? What did those numbers actually do for you — in practice?"
- "When did tracking feel like support, and when did it feel like judgement?"
- "If something could tell you how a meal went — without using any numbers — what would you want to feel or know?"

---

**Interview notes:**
- If a participant pauses, wait at least 5 seconds before prompting.
- Follow-up with: *"Could you tell me a bit more about that?"* — avoid leading prompts like *"So do you mean...?"*
- Never express surprise or evaluative reactions to what participants share.

## Phase 2 — One-Day Photo Diary
*Self-administered by participants. No researcher questions during Phase 2.*

**Instructions given to participants in advance (printed card or sent as message):**

---
> **Post-Meal Photo Diary — Instructions**
>
> On the agreed day, within **15–25 minutes** after each main meal:
>
> **① Take one photo of your post-meal environment**
> Don't photograph the food itself — photograph the space: empty plates, the table, your hands, where you're sitting, what's around you.
>
> **② Write 3 short lines on your phone:**
> · Where did you eat, and what were you doing at the same time?
> · How does your body feel right now?
>   *(You can use words like: heavy / light / full / warm / settled / restless / satisfied / empty / calm / anxious…)*
> · Compared to right after you finished eating, has anything shifted in how your body feels?
>
> Send everything to the shared album / directly to me via message.
> Thank you!

---

*Researcher's task during Phase 2: Send a brief reminder before each meal if needed. Collect all photos and text. Print each person's photos in chronological order (4 per A4 page) with their written notes alongside. These printouts are used as the opening materials in Phase 4.*

## Phase 3 — Group Lunch Session

**Total: ~60 minutes · 4–5 participants**

---

### Pre-meal written questionnaire (0:00–0:05)
*Participants write their answers individually on paper — not discussed aloud.*

1. How hungry are you right now? *(0 = not hungry at all · 10 = very hungry)*: \_\_\_
2. Are you looking forward to this meal? *(yes / not particularly / no)*: \_\_\_
3. How are you feeling today? *(one word)*: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

---

### Shared meal — no structured activity (0:05–0:30)
*Participants eat their own lunch together. Researcher observes without intervening.*

*Researcher notes (written, not spoken): Who eats quickly vs. slowly? Who looks at their phone while eating? Who pays attention to what they're eating? Any visible signs of self-monitoring?*

---

### Facilitated post-meal group discussion (0:30–0:50)
*Target: within the 20-minute post-meal satiety window (Robinson et al., 2014). Participants take turns — no pressure to respond.*

**Opening:**
> "The meal's done. Let's stay here for a bit and see what happens. How does your body feel right now? Take a moment, and describe it in a few words."
*(Go around the table.)*

**Follow-up questions:**

> "Was there a moment during the meal when you felt 'that's enough'? Or did you just keep eating until your plate was empty?"

> "If there was a gentle prompt — maybe a vibration on your phone, or a soft light — 20 minutes after you finished eating, asking you to pause and notice how your body feels: no score, no judgement. Would you stop and check in with yourself, or would you ignore it?"

> "Do you ever think about whether you 'ate well' after a meal? What does that thought feel like?"

---

### Optional: Design concept preview (0:50–1:00)
*Only if time allows and the group seems engaged.*

> "I want to show you a quick sketch of something I'm designing — what's your first reaction?"
*(Show Concept B sketch: a post-meal 20-minute body-check prompt.)*

## Phase 4 — Design Probe Interviews

**Total: ~40 minutes · Individual · Phase 1/2 participants**

---

### Part 1: Opening with the participant's own diary (10 minutes)
*Lay their printed photo diary on the table.*

> "I printed out the photos you sent me. Let's look at them together — what's your first reaction seeing them?"

> "Which photo feels most different from how your body actually felt at that moment?"

---

### Part 2: Concept A — Food Environment Structuring (10 minutes)
*Show hand-drawn sketch: meal plate with food separated into sections (vegetables / protein / carbohydrates); eating sequence suggestion; phone placed away from the table.*

> "If your plate or eating setup automatically separated your food like this, do you think it would change how you eat?"

> "Does this feel like support — or like being controlled?"

> "Is this realistic in your daily life? Which part might you actually try? Which part would feel like a hassle or unnecessary?"

> "What would make you not do this?"

---

### Part 3: Concept B — Post-Meal Body-Awareness Prompt (10 minutes)
*Show hand-drawn sketch: 20 minutes after a meal, a gentle prompt (phone vibration / soft light) invites you to pause and check in with your body. No score, no rating.*

> "Twenty minutes after a meal, a gentle prompt asks you to pause and notice how your body feels — no score, no judgement. Would you stop and check in, or would you ignore it?"

> "Does this feel like care — or surveillance?"

> "If this existed, what form would you want it to take? A phone notification? A physical object? Something else?"

---

### Part 4: Open questions (10 minutes)

> "If you could design something to help your relationship with food feel better, what would you want it to do?"

> "What is the one thing you absolutely would not want it to do?"

---

## Researcher Notes (for all phases)

- Use *"Could you tell me a bit more about that?"* as the default follow-up. Avoid leading questions.
- If a participant pauses, wait at least 5 seconds.
- Do not react with surprise, approval, or disapproval to anything shared.
- Do not use the words "good," "bad," "healthy," or "unhealthy" when describing food.
- If a participant seems uncomfortable at any point, acknowledge it and offer the option to skip or stop.
