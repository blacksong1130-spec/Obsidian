## Abstract 摘要

When a self-monitoring tool is used to enhance behavior awareness, the tool should afford reflection by design. This work examines the “valence of meal” (i.e., healthy versus unhealthy meal) as a means to support reflection on a person’s diet in photo-based meal tracking. To study the effect of imposing valence on meal tracking, we designed two conditions—one focusing on capturing healthy meals, the other capturing unhealthy meals—and conducted a between-subjects diary study with 22 college students over four weeks. According to their group assignment, participants tracked only healthy or unhealthy meals by taking photos and rationalizing in texts why their meals were particularly healthy or unhealthy. We found that participants in both groups became more aware of their diet, but the valence of meal influenced them differently regarding their meal assessment, self-reflection, and food choice intention. We discuss ways to leverage valence in designing reflective meal tracking systems.  
当使用自我监测工具提升行为意识时，该工具应设计成能够进行反思。本研究考察了“餐食价值”（即健康餐与不健康餐）作为支持基于照片的饮食追踪反思的手段。为了研究加价对饮食追踪的影响，我们设计了两种条件——一种侧重于捕捉健康餐食，另一种用于捕捉不健康餐食——并对22名大学生进行了为期四周的受试者间日记研究。根据小组作业，参与者只通过拍照和在文字中合理化为何餐食特别健康或不健康来追踪健康或不健康的食物。我们发现两组参与者对自己的饮食意识有所提升，但餐食的价值在他们的餐食评估、自我反思和食物选择意图方面产生了不同的影响。我们讨论了如何利用价值来设计反射式饮食追踪系统。

## Introduction 简介

Food tracking has been a prevalent approach for discovering personal dietary insights [^17] [^23] [^46]. In food tracking, *reflection* is critical for developing and sustaining healthy eating habits, because reflecting on the experience helps people be aware of their diet, think about their food practices, and potentially make better food choices [^15] [^29]. Despite the burden associated with food tracking and reflection, it stands to benefit those who are concerned with their eating habits. In this work, we aim to make food tracking a reflective practice by having people pass judgment on their meals and capture their reasoning, rather than logging the breakdown of food components, as in traditional food tracking.  
食物追踪一直是获取个人饮食洞察 [^17] [^23] [^46] 的一种常见方法。在食物追踪中， *反思* 对于培养和维持健康饮食习惯至关重要，因为反思经历帮助人们意识到自己的饮食，思考饮食习惯，并可能做出 [^15] [^29] 更好的饮食选择。尽管食物追踪和反思带来负担，但这对关注自己饮食习惯的人来说是有益的。在这项工作中，我们旨在通过让人们对自己的餐食做出评判并记录他们的推理，从而使食物追踪成为一种反思性实践，而不是像传统食物追踪那样记录食物成分的分解。

In supporting reflective food tracking experience, we are inspired by prior work on the framing effects generated by valence manipulation. Tversky and Kahneman introduced the “decision frame,” a decision maker’s conception of acts, the outcomes, and the contingencies of a certain decision [^50]. *Valence,* a type of frame manipulation [^35], is recognized as a way to differently shape people’s perceptions on the same information. An example of valence manipulation is highlighting either a positive or negative aspect of individual performance [^19] [^34] or information being conveyed [^18] [^32]. Choosing more powerful valence has shown to effectively influence people’s decision making [^32], improve self-efficacy [^19], and induce behavior change [^34]. In this work, we examine how the valence of tracking condition (i.e., tracking healthy meals versus unhealthy meals) affects people’s daily meal tracking experience, including how they assess the healthiness of meal, reflect on their food practice, and intend to make food choice.  
在支持反思性食物追踪体验时，我们受到了先前关于价交换操作所产生的框架效应研究的启发。特沃斯基和卡尼曼引入了“决策框架”，即决策者对行为、结果及某一决定 [^50] 的偶然性的看法。 *价值是一种* 框架操控 [^35] ，被认为是用来改变人们对同一信息感知的方式。价值操作的一个例子是突出个体表现 [^19] [^34] 或传递信息的正面或负面方面 [^18] [^32] 。选择更强效价已被证明能有效影响人们的 [^32] 决策，提升自我效能 [^19] 感，并诱导行为改变 [^34] 。本研究探讨了追踪健康状况（即追踪健康餐与不健康餐食）的价值如何影响人们的日常餐食记录体验，包括他们如何评估餐食健康、反思自身饮食习惯以及如何做出食物选择。

We also leverage photo-based tracking in our study. Echoed by the phrase, “a picture is worth a thousand words,” food images can serve as visual prompts for reflection. Photo-based tracking has been recognized for its potential to mitigate challenges in traditional tracking methods [^21] [^23] [^38] [^46] such as by lowering tracking burden [^24] [^30] [^52], enabling photo sharing [^21], providing contextual information [^24], and enhancing self-awareness [^25] [^41]. In this work, we adopt photo-based tracking to examine how people reflect on meal healthiness *in-situ* using Microsoft’s OneNote mobile app [^7].  
我们还在研究中利用了基于照片的追踪技术。正如“一图胜千言”这句话所呼应，食物图片可以作为视觉提示，促使人们反思。基于照片的追踪因其降低追踪负担 [^24] [^30] [^52] 、支持照片共享 [^21] 、提供上下文信息 [^24] 以及增强自我意识等传统追踪方法 [^21] [^23] [^38] [^46] 挑战的潜力而受到认可 [^25] [^41] 。本研究采用基于照片的追踪，研究人们 *如何利用 Microsoft* 的 OneNote 移动应用 [^7] 现场反思餐食健康状况。

We conducted a between-subjects diary study with 22 college students over four weeks. According to their group assignment, participants were asked to track only healthy or unhealthy meals by taking photos and rationalizing in texts why their meals were healthy or unhealthy. During the study, we collected a rich collection of *situated data* including meal photos, assessment on meal healthiness, reflection texts, and usage logs. Although the goal of this study is not to change people’s eating behavior, people could become more aware of their habits through self-monitoring [^42]. In fact, we design the study to promote active reflection by way of taking photos and assessing their meals, which could affect their eating intentions. In the following, we summarize related work, and describe our research question and study method. Then, we report the results from the diary study followed by discussion on ways to design reflective food tracking systems.  
我们对 22 名大学生进行了为期四周的学科间日记研究。根据小组作业，参与者被要求只通过拍照和在短信中合理化自己餐食健康或不健康的理由来跟踪健康或不健康的食物。研究期间，我们收集了丰富的 *情境数据* ，包括餐食照片、饮食健康评估、反思文本和使用日志。虽然本研究的目标不是改变人们的饮食行为，但人们可以通过自我监测 [^42] 来更加意识到自己的习惯。事实上，我们设计这项研究是为了通过拍照和评估他们的饮食来促进积极反思，这可能影响他们的饮食意图。以下我们将总结相关工作，并描述我们的研究问题和研究方法。随后，我们报告日记研究结果，随后讨论如何设计反思性食品追踪系统。

## Related Work 相关工作

In this section, we cover related work in the areas of reflective tracking, food tracking, and framing research. Built upon previous research, we highlight theoretical and empirical implications that motivate our work.  
本节将介绍反思追踪、食物追踪和框架研究领域的相关工作。基于以往研究，我们强调了激励我们工作的理论和实证影响。

### Reflective Tracking 反射跟踪

According to Moon, reflection is a purposeful analysis of a person’s own knowledge and experience as a means of achieving deeper meaning and understanding [^40]. In Li’s stage-based model of personal informatics, reflection is the stage where people have finished integrating their data and are about to take corresponding action [^36]. Schon further describes reflection as occurring in two processes: reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action. Reflection-in-action is seen as thinking about an event in-situ (i.e., at the time it occurs). Whereas, reflection-on-action refers to learning from thoughts and experiences after the event [^47]. In the context of self-tracking, Ploderer and colleagues further specify how these two modes of reflection can be supported by providing different types of feedback: reflection-inaction through real-time feedback and reflection-on-action through aggregated feedback [^44]. While reflection-on-action supports learning from past choices retroactively, reflection-in-action can benefit situations as they are occurring, which can lead to more responsive decisions [^47]. Applying this concept to meal tracking, we see two opportune moments to encourage reflective meal tracking: (1) the time of capturing a meal and (2) the time of reviewing meal records. In our study, we designed the meal tracking regimen with an aim to support both reflection-in-action (by asking people to capture specific valence of meal and rationalize in text) and reflection-on-action (by providing weekly feedback).  
Moon 认为，反思是一种有目的地分析个人知识和经验，以实现更深层的意义和理解 [^40] 。在李的基于阶段的个人信息学模型中，反思是指人们完成数据整合并准备采取相应行动 [^36] 的阶段。Schon 进一步将反思描述为两个过程：行动中的反思和行动中的反思。行动中的反思被视为在事件发生时（即事件发生时）思考。而行动反思则指事后从思想和经验中学习 [^47] 。在自我跟踪的背景下，Ploderer 及其同事进一步说明了如何通过提供不同类型的反馈来支持这两种反思模式：通过实时反馈实现反思-不作为，以及通过汇总反馈实现的对行动反思 [^44] 。行动反思支持从过去选择中事后学习，而行动反思则能在情境发生时提供帮助，从而促使决策更具响应 [^47] 性。将这一概念应用于餐食记录，我们看到两个鼓励反思性餐食跟踪的好时机：（1）记录餐食的时间和（2）回顾餐食记录的时间。在我们的研究中，我们设计了饮食追踪方案，旨在支持行动中的反思（通过让人们捕捉具体的用餐价值并在文字中进行合理化）和行动反思（通过每周反馈）。

One of the leading reasons for personal data tracking is to change or maintain his or her behavior through self-reflection [^37]. As such, supporting technology mediated reflection is often considered an important goal in designing self-tracking tools [^13] [^20] [^33] [^37]. For example, Choe and colleagues leveraged visual data exploration in facilitating self-reflection and found that visual data exploration enables people to identify numerous personal data insights [^20]. Isaacs and colleagues designed a reflection tool that supports capturing personal experience and emotional state. They learned that recording and reflecting on the captured data could enhance personal well-being [^33]. In light of this prior work, we aim to study the effect of valence on people’s reflection in the context of meal tracking.  
个人数据追踪的主要原因之一是通过自我反思来改变或维持自己的行为 [^37] 。因此，支持技术介导反思常被视为设计自我追踪工具 [^13] [^20] [^33] [^37] 的重要目标。例如，Choe 及其同事利用视觉数据探索促进自我反思，发现视觉数据探索使人们能够识别大量个人数据洞察 [^20] 。Isaacs 及其同事设计了一种反思工具，支持捕捉个人经历和情绪状态。他们了解到，记录和反思捕获的数据可以提升个人福祉 [^33] 。基于此前的研究，我们旨在研究价值对人们在饮食追踪中反思能力的影响。

### Food Tracking 食品追踪

As food choices play an important role in an individual’s everyday life, many commercial applications (e.g., Make My Plate [^4], MyFitnessPal [^5], MyFoodDiary [^6], Bitesnap [^2], YouAte [^9]) are marketed to support people track their daily meals. Most of the application designs are driven by consumer demands such as losing weight or watching calories. Meanwhile, research studies have focused on supporting people’s healthy eating goals [^43], enhancing self awareness [^14], and promoting positive behavior change [^21] through food tracking. Additionally, researchers have employed different strategies to nudge healthy food choices and enhance user engagement by showing visual feedback [^15], applying photo-based tracking [^24] [^30] [^52], providing nutrition knowledge [^26] [^28], and enabling social sharing [^17]. As food tracking typically imposes high capture burden, researchers developed automatic food tracking mechanisms [^12] [^48]. However, the automated approach often limits opportunities for self-reflection. In this work, we adopt photo-based food tracking to alleviate the tracking burden while engaging people in the data capture process to provide opportunities for self-reflection. Note that we use food tracking as a way to understand people’s reasoning and reflection on the healthiness of meals, rather than to collect food items per se. Although prior works have examined people’s healthiness decisions, relying on surveys and interviews [^11] [^22] [^27], our study goes further by collecting contextualized responses in a diary study.  
由于食物选择在个人日常生活中扮演重要角色，许多商业应用（如 Make My Plate [^4] 、MyFitnessPal [^5] 、MyFoodDiary [^6] 、Bitesnap [^2] 、YouAte [^9] ）被推向帮助人们追踪每日餐食。大多数应用设计都受消费者需求驱动，比如减肥或控制卡路里。与此同时，研究重点关注支持人们的健康饮食目标 [^43] 、增强自我认知 [^14] ，以及通过食物追踪促进积极行为改变 [^21] 。此外，研究人员采用了多种策略，通过展示视觉反馈 [^15] 、应用基于照片的追踪 [^24] [^30] [^52] 、提供营养知识 [^26] [^28] 以及促进社交分享 [^17] ，推动健康饮食选择并提升用户参与度。由于食物追踪通常带来较高的捕获负担，研究人员开发了 [^12] [^48] 自动食物追踪机制。然而，自动化方法往往限制了自我反思的机会。在这项工作中，我们采用基于照片的食物追踪来减轻追踪负担，同时让人们参与数据采集过程，提供自我反思的机会。请注意，我们使用食物追踪来理解人们对餐食健康的推理和反思，而不是单纯收集食物。尽管以往的研究依赖调查和访谈来考察人们的健康决策 [^11] [^22] [^27] ，但我们的研究更进一步，通过日记研究收集了情境化的回答。

### Framing Research 框架研究

Researchers have examined the effect of valence manipulation on self-efficacy and behavior in different contexts [^19] [^32] [^34]. To test performance under different framing, Hwang and colleagues represented food choices using different monster avatars, which led participants to pay more attention to nutrient information: When positive messages were paired with negative visuals, participants exhibited healthier choices [^32]. To investigate which framing of individuals’ performance leads to higher self-efficacy in achieving health activity goals, Choe and colleagues compared *remaining framing* (i.e., amount left to achieve the goal) and *achieved framing* (i.e., amount achieved toward the goal), and found that achieved framing leads to an increased self-efficacy [^19]. Similarly, Kim and colleagues designed *TimeAware*, a desktop-based system with productivity feedback on positive framing (i.e., productivity level) and negative framing (i.e., distraction level). They found that negative framing leads to better work productivity, but caused more stress [^34].  
研究人员研究了价性操作对不同情境下自我效能感和行为的影响 [^19] [^32] [^34] 。为了测试不同框架下的表现，黄博士及其同事用不同的怪物头像来表示食物选择，这使参与者更加关注营养信息：当正面信息与负面视觉搭配时，参与者表现出更健康的选择 [^32] 。为了探究哪种表现框架能带来更高的自我效能感，Choe 及其同事比较 *了剩余* 框架（即剩余的目标目标）和 *已实现框架* （即实现目标的目标的数量），发现实现框架的自我效能感会提高 [^19] 。同样，Kim 及其同事设计了 *TimeAware* ，这是一个基于桌面的系统，能够对积极框架（即生产力水平）和负面框架（即分心水平）进行生产力反馈。他们发现负面框架能提高工作效率，但会带来更多压力 [^34] 。

In the context of food research, although not directly referring to meal valence, studies have examined people’s perception on healthy versus unhealthy meals in different ways. For example, Calitri and colleagues conducted a one-year longitudinal study to investigate people’s cognitive bias towards healthy or unhealthy food, and the effects of bias on their eating behaviors. They found that the bias to unhealthy foods could predict an increase in Body Mass Index (BMI), whereas bias to healthy foods could predict a decrease in BMI [^16]. In another study, researchers adopted the strategy of implementation intention to ask people to replace unhealthy snacks with healthy snacks and specify when, where, and why they did so. They found that *motivational cues* to inquire why foods were consumed decreased unhealthy snack consumption more than *situational cues* asking where and when [^10]. Similar to this work, we ask participants to rationalize “why” they think a particular meal item is healthy or unhealthy as a prompting cue.  
在食品研究的背景下，虽然不直接指餐值，但研究以不同方式探讨了人们对健康餐食与不健康餐食的看法。例如，Calitri 及其同事进行了一项为期一年的纵向研究，调查人们对健康或不健康食物的认知偏见及其对饮食行为的影响。他们发现，对不健康食物的偏见可能预测身体质量指数（BMI）的上升，而对健康食物的偏见则可能预测 BMI 的下降 [^16] 。在另一项研究中，研究人员采用了实施意图策略，要求人们用健康零食替代不健康的零食，并具体说明何时、何地以及为何这样做。他们发现，询问食物为何被消费的 *动机线索* ，比询问何时 [^10] 何地的 *情境线索* 更能减少不健康零食的摄入。与这项工作类似，我们要求参与者合理化“为什么”他们认为某一餐类健康或不健康，作为提示。

## Research Questions and Study Instruments研究问题与学习工具

We postulate that the valence of meals could shape people’s food tracking experiences. Thus, we aim to answer the following research question (RQ):  
我们推测，餐食的价值可能会影响人们的食物追踪体验。因此，我们旨在回答以下研究问题（RQ）：

> How does the valence of meals people capture affect their food tracking experience, including their assessment on meal healthiness, self-reflection, and food choice intention?  
> 人们捕捉到的食物价值如何影响他们的食物追踪体验，包括对饮食健康的评估、自我反思和食物选择意图？

To examine this RQ, we designed a between-subjects diary study with two meal tracking conditions— *healthy meal tracking group* (HG) and *unhealthy meal tracking group* (UG) [^1]. Depending on the group assignment, we asked participants to track the meals they considered healthy (HG) or unhealthy (UG) by taking meal photos, and to rationalize in texts their reasons for considering each meal as healthy or unhealthy.  
为分析这一 RQ，我们设计了一项受试者间日记研究，采用两种饮食跟踪条件—— *健康饮食跟踪组* （HG）和 *不健康饮食跟踪组* （UG）。 [^1] 根据小组作业的不同，我们要求参与者通过拍摄餐食照片来追踪他们认为健康（HG）或不健康（UG）的餐食，并在文本中合理化他们认为每餐健康或不健康的理由。

For data capturing, we used Microsoft’s OneNote mobile app [^7], which is a flexible note taking app that supports capturing texts and photos. OneNote also supports sharing notes, allowing each participant to share their meal tracking entries with the research team in real time. For each meal entry, we asked participants to include the following information as comments: meal type (i.e., breakfast, lunch, dinner, snack), meal name (e.g., salad), key items (e.g., salad with tomato and lettuce), preparation method (i.e., homemade, pre-packaged, or restaurant), and the reason why the participant considered it healthy (or unhealthy). Participants were instructed to include their index finger next to the meal, within the the photo, as a reference of portion size. An example of a complete entry is shown in ([Figure 1](#f1-2976906)). In this paper, we use “OneNote Meal” to denote this meal tracking regimen facilitated by the OneNote mobile app.

We provided the top 6 nutritional facts commonly considered: calorie, protein, total carbohydrate, sugar, dietary fiber, fat (total fat and saturated fat), and sodium [^3]. Our primary source of nutritional information was Supertracker [^8], a resource approved by United States Department of Agriculture (USDA). If the meal information was not on Super-tracker, we turned to MyFitnessPal, and the websites of relevant restaurants or recipes, if any. The weekly summary was designed to promote participant interpretation, rather than directly indicating the healthiness of their meals.

## Method 方法

We conducted a between-subjects diary study for four weeks. To recruit participants, we distributed the recruitment material through the university research volunteer website. The study occurred at a U.S. Mid-Atlantic state university.  
我们进行了为期四周的受试者间日记研究。为了招募参与者，我们通过大学研究志愿者网站分发了招募材料。该研究在美国中大西洋地区的一所州立大学进行。

### Study Procedure 学习流程

Participants were randomly assigned to one of the two groups (HG or UG). Before the four-week diary study, we led one-on-one pre-study sessions. During the 30-minute pre-study session, we described the study procedure and obtained participant consent. We helped them install Microsoft’s OneNote app on their mobile phone, created a OneNote notebook for them to track meals, and provided detailed instructions and examples of meal entries. Right after the pre-study session, we asked participants to complete a short pre-study survey, which included a demographic inquiry, a self-reported healthy eating level (i.e., from 1 for far below the average to 5 for far above the average), and a dietary quality questionnaire. We designed this questionnaire through a modified survey extracted from valid Food Frequency Questionnaires (FFQ) [^31] [^39] [^49] [^51]. The modification of this survey was advised by a registered dietitian as to ensure its accuracy and relevance to the college-age population and their most commonly faced dietary decisions.  
参与者被随机分配到两组之一（HG 组或 UG）。在为期四周的日记研究之前，我们进行了一对一的预复习。在 30 分钟的预研课中，我们介绍了研究流程并获得了参与者同意。我们帮助他们在手机上安装了 Microsoft 的 OneNote 应用，创建了一个用来记录餐食的 OneNote 笔记本，并提供了详细的说明和餐食条目示例。预研会结束后，我们要求参与者填写简短的预研究问卷，包括人口统计调查、自我报告的健康饮食水平（即远低于平均值为 1，远高于平均值为 5），以及饮食质量问卷。我们通过从有效的食物频率问卷（FFQ） [^31] [^39] [^49] [^51] 中提取的修改版问卷设计了本问卷。该调查的修改由注册营养师建议，以确保其准确性及与大学生年龄人群及其最常面临的饮食决策相关。

If participants believed they did not have anything healthy (or unhealthy) for a particular day, we asked them to submit a short comment indicating that they did not have anything to capture. We asked participants to submit at least one entry per day. They could submit multiple entries if they considered more than one of their meals to be healthy (or unhealthy) in a day. If a participant did not submit any entries for a day, we sent them an email reminder. If a participant missed entries for 3 consecutive days or more, they were dropped from the study.  
如果参与者认为某天没有健康（或不健康）的内容，我们要求他们提交简短评论，说明他们没有任何可捕捉的内容。我们要求参与者每天至少提交一篇作品。如果他们认为一天内有多餐是健康（或不健康）的，可以提交多个作品。如果参与者一天内未提交任何作品，我们会发送电子邮件提醒。如果参与者连续3天或以上未提交，则会被剔除出研究。

At the end of each week, we sent participants in both groups a weekly summary of the meals they had captured, with the nutritional information of each food item ([Figure 2](#f2-2976906)) over the past week. After four weeks, participants took a post-study survey, which included the same dietary quality questionnaire used in the pre-study survey. We then conducted a 30-minute post-study interview with each participant to understand their meal tracking experiences, particularly their assessment on meal healthiness, self-reflection, and food choice intention. We audio-recorded and transcribed all of the interviews. After the study, each participant received a $20 gift card or cash based on their preference.  
每周结束时，我们向两组参与者发送了他们捕获的餐食每周摘要，并附有过去一周内每项食物的营养信息（ [见图 2](#f2-2976906) ）。四周后，参与者进行了研究后调查，调查中包含了与研究前调查相同的饮食质量问卷。随后，我们对每位参与者进行了 30 分钟的访谈，了解他们的饮食追踪体验，特别是他们对饮食健康、自我反思和食物选择意图的评估。我们对所有采访进行了录音和转录。研究结束后，每位参与者根据喜好获得一张 20 美元的礼品卡或现金。

### Data Analysis 数据分析

From the pre- and post-study survey data, we calculated participants’ pre- and post-study dietary quality scores and their self-reported healthy eating levels. We also analyzed the descriptive data on entry types (e.g., entries with photos and comments, entries with comment-only) and frequencies of each type (see [Table 1](#t1-2976906)). For qualitative data from written comments and interview transcripts, we used bottom-up thematic analysis; three researchers, together, iteratively coded interview transcripts to identify emerging patterns and discussed when there were any discrepancies.  
根据研究前后调查数据，我们计算了参与者的研究前后饮食质量评分及其自我报告的健康饮食水平。我们还分析了关于条目类型的描述性数据（例如带照片和注释的条目，仅有注释的条目）以及每种类型的出现频率（见 [表 1](#t1-2976906) ）。对于来自书面评论和访谈记录的定性数据，我们采用了自下而上的主题分析;三位研究人员共同反复编码访谈记录以识别新出现的模式，并在出现不一致时讨论。

## Results 选举结果

We recruited 29 participants, and all of them were individuals who (1) were at least 18 years old; (2) had a smart phone that could install the OneNote app; (3) had data plan or WiFi access; and (4) had no history of eating disorders. At the end of the study, 22 (13 in HG, 9 in UG; 16 female, 6 male) out of 29 (75.9%) participants completed all the study activities. These participants’ ages ranged from 18 to 29 (*M* = 21.64, *SD* = 2.56). We note that our participants were limited to healthy college students in the U.S. and were predominantly women (72.7%). Thus, our results may not generalize beyond this group. Participants came from diverse backgrounds; nine of them were international students (4 Chinese, 3 Korean, and 2 Indian), and thirteen were domestic U.S. students. Participants were 1 freshman, 3 sophomores, 8 juniors, 5 seniors, and 5 graduate students (2 M.S. students and 3 Ph.D. students). Seven participants dropped out of the study—6 participants who did not meet the minimum data tracking requirement (4 HG, 2 UG) and 1 HG participant for personal reasons. Among the seven participants who dropped out of the study, five of them participated in part over the winter and spring break, which could have led to low adherence to the study regimen.  
我们招募了 29 名参与者，所有参与者均为（1）年满 18 岁的个体;（2）拥有一部可以安装 OneNote 应用的智能手机;（3）拥有数据套餐或 WiFi 访问;以及（4）无饮食失调病史。研究结束时，29 名参与者中有 22 人（13 人为 HG，9 人为 UG;16 人为女性，6 人为男性），75.9%完成了所有研究活动。这些参与者的年龄范围为 18 至 29 岁（ *M* = 21.64，SD = 2.56）。 我们注意到，参与者仅限于美国健康大学生，且女性占 72.7%。因此，我们的结果可能无法推广到该群体之外。参与者来自多元背景;其中 9 名是国际学生（4 名中国学生，3 名韩国学生，2 名印度学生），13 名是美国国内学生。参与者包括 1 名大一新生、3 名大二学生、8 名大三学生、5 名大四学生和 5 名研究生（2 名硕士生和 3 名博士生）。7 名参与者退出了研究——其中 6 名未达到最低数据跟踪要求的参与者（4 名 HG，2 名 UG），1 名因个人原因退出 HG。在七名退出研究的参与者中，有五人在寒假和春假期间部分参与，这可能导致对研究方案的依从性较低。

In the following, we report our findings in four categories: general usage, effect of valence on participants’ assessment of meal, effect of valence on participants’ self-reflection, and effect of valence on participants’ food choice intention.  
以下我们将发现分为四类：一般使用、价值对参与者对餐食评估的影响、价值对参与者自我反思的影响，以及价值对参与者食物选择意图的影响。

### General Usage 一般用法

Over the course of four-week study, 22 participants logged 642 entries (*M* = 29.18, *SD* = 8.32) in total. On average, HG participants missed 3.69 days and UG participants missed 4 days of logging. Although HG participants submitted more entries (*M* = 31.1, *SD* = 9.44) than UG participants (*M* = 26.3, *SD* = 5.70), we found no significant difference between the two groups. Details of OneNote Meal entries are shown in [Table 1](#t1-2976906). Regarding dietary quality scores collected via pre and post study survey, participants in both groups showed a slight increase in their dietary quality, but the change was not significant. For self-reported healthy eating level, five participants (H1, H2, H11, U3, U8) reported an increase in their healthy eating level, and five (H3, H5, H7, U2, U4) reported a decrease in their healthy eating level. In what follows, we describe the results according to our research question.  
在为期四周的研究中，共有 22 名参与者共记录了 642 条条目（ *M* = 29.18， *标准差* = 8.32）。平均而言，HG 参与者缺席 3.69 天，本科组缺席 4 天。尽管 HG 参与者提交的条目数量 *多于 UG* 组（ *M* = 26.3，SD = 5.70），但两组之间无显著差异。 OneNote 餐点的详细信息见 [表 1](#t1-2976906) 。关于通过研究前后调查收集的饮食质量评分，两组参与者的饮食质量均略有提升，但变化不显著。在自我报告的健康饮食水平方面，五名参与者（H1、H2、H11、U3、U8）报告健康饮食水平有所提升，五名参与者（H3、H5、H7、U2、U4）报告健康饮食水平下降。接下来我们将根据研究问题描述这些结果。

### Effect on Assessing the Healthiness of Meals对评估食品健康性的影响

When participants assessed their meal, they used heuristics such as nutrition components, meal composition, preparation methods, food items, brand stereotypes, and social influences. It is worth noting that, although participants in both groups used these heuristics, how they applied them differed by their tracking condition.  
在评估餐食时，参与者采用了启发式方法，如营养成分、餐食组成、准备方法、食品、品牌刻板印象和社会影响。值得注意的是，尽管两组参与者都使用了这些启发式方法，但他们的应用方式因追踪条件而异。

It came prominently that participants assigned to the healthy meal tracking group tended to focus on the healthy side of their meals, whereas those in the unhealthy meal tracking group tended to focus on the unhealthy side of their meals. When referring to the meal composition, HG participants tended to consider a meal healthy if *“the majority of the items on the plate were considered healthy”* (HG-1). In some occasions, the addition of one more healthy item could make an entire meal healthy despite other unhealthy items: *“adding an egg to a noodle dish makes for a healthy meal because of added protein”* (HG-12). Other times, HG participants also used the overall food variety to signify that they had a healthy meal: *“I like to have the whole meal with mixed colors”* (HG-4). In contrast to HG participants, UG participants tended to consider a meal unhealthy even when there was only a few unhealthy components, such as dessert (UG-1,3,4,7,8), or unhealthy ingredients such as sugar (UG-3,7,8,9) and preservatives (UG-6). For example, UG-3 noted in their OneNote that *“this meal was unhealthy because of the icecream,”* and UG-6 explained that *“the sauce is from a jar, it has preservatives and unnatural ingredients that would be better offleft out ofmy diet*.”  
值得注意的是，被分配到健康饮食追踪组的参与者倾向于关注餐食的健康面，而不健康餐食追踪组的参与者则更倾向于关注不健康的餐食。在谈及餐食组成时，HG 参与者倾向于认为“ *盘子上大多数食物都被认为是健康的”* （HG-1）。在某些情况下，再多加一种健康食材，即使有其他不健康的食物，整顿饭也能保持健康：“ *在面条中加入鸡蛋，因为增加了蛋白质，这也成了一顿健康的餐食”* （HG-12）。有时，HG 参与者还用整体食物多样性来表示他们吃了健康的一顿饭：“ *我喜欢整顿带有混合色彩的餐食”* （HG-4）。与 HG 参与者不同，UG 参与者往往认为一餐即使只有少量不健康成分，如甜点（UG-1,3,4,7,8）或糖（UG-3,7,8,9）和防腐剂（UG-6）等不健康成分。例如，UG-3 在他们的 OneNote 中指出 *“这顿饭因为冰淇淋不健康”，* 而 UG-6 解释 *说“酱料来自罐子里，含有防腐剂和非天然成分，最好从我的饮食中剔除* 。”

This finding was further exemplified by our observations where participants in both groups captured similar food items, but perceived the healthiness of the meal differently. For example, HG-7 thought that quesadilla was healthy due to its variety of food groups ([Figure 3](#f3-2976906) -a), whereas UG-8 thought the opposite because of an unbalanced ratio between healthy (i.e., veggie) and unhealthy (i.e., cheese) components ([Figure 3](#f3-2976906) -b). Other types of meals, such as an omelette and pasta, were also frequently captured by both groups, with opposite reasoning.  
这一发现还通过观察得到进一步体现：两组参与者捕捉的食物相似，但对餐食健康性的感知不同。例如，HG-7 认为墨西哥饼因其食物类别多样而健康（ [图 3-a](#f3-2976906) ），而 UG-8 则相反，因为健康（即蔬菜）与不健康（如奶酪）成分比例不平衡（ [图 3-b](#f3-2976906) ）。其他类型的餐食，如煎蛋卷和意面，也经常被两组人共同掌握，理由相反。

### Effect on Self-Reflection对自我反思的影响

Participants in both groups acknowledged that they had gained awareness of their diet—especially whether they were eating healthy or unhealthy: *“I think it was helpful to keep track of foods and I realized what I was eating, especially when I had to take pictures of what I thought was healthy…”* (HG-3). UG participants also expressed that tracking unhealthy meals helped them find patterns in their daily meals (UG-2). For HG participants, tracking healthy food painted a brighter picture of their diet, and therefore helped them with their self esteem. This was echoed in a statement from HG-4: *“If you only track healthy parts \[of your meals,\] you probably feel a lot better about yourself rather than \[tracking\] everything”.* For participants who had a wide range of healthy and unhealthy meals, tracking healthy meals made them pay more attention to their healthy meals and feel positive about themselves. Notably, some HG participants expressed a sense of guilt when there was nothing healthy to capture (HG-3, HG-5, HG-11). For example, HG-5 considered eating healthy meals as a daily task; on the days when there was nothing to capture, they felt *“a little frustrated, a little mad because I didn’t accomplish the task \[…\] it became a task for myself.”*  
两组参与者都承认他们对自己的饮食有了意识——尤其是对自己是健康还是不健康饮食的认知：“ *我认为记录食物很有帮助，我意识到自己吃了什么，尤其是在不得不拍摄我认为健康的食物时......*”（HG-3）。本科参与者还表示，追踪不健康饮食有助于他们发现每日饮食的规律（UG-2）。对于健康饮食参与者来说，追踪健康食物能让他们的饮食形象更为光明，从而提升了自尊心。HG-4 的声明也呼应了这一点：“ *如果你只记录健康的餐食部分，你可能会对自己感觉更好，而不是事事* 俱进。”对于那些吃了各种健康和不健康餐食的参与者，追踪健康餐食让他们更加关注健康餐食，并对自己感到积极。值得注意的是，一些 HG 参与者在没有健康可捕捉的对象时表现出内疚感（HG-3、HG-5、HG-11）。例如，HG-5 将健康饮食视为每日任务;在没有什么可捕捉的日子里，他们会感到 *“有点沮丧，有点生气，因为我没完成任务\[...\]这成了我自己的一项任务。”*

Compared with HG participants, UG participants were more surprised to realize that their meals were quite unhealthy (UG-6, UG-7). For example, UG-7 noted, *“I like to think of myself as a healthy eater \[…\] but I realize that a lot of things that I thought I have every now and then are a lot more unhealthy than I thought they were. I guess consciously thinking about \[them\], and having to document everything really made me realize.”* UG-6 also expressed: *“I think I never realized how much cheese I eat. I noticed that I’m putting a lot of extra fat on everything I eat.”* UG participants (UG-3, UG-5, UG-7) also expressed a sense of guilt, but in a way opposite from HG participants; they felt guilty when they captured unhealthy meals and felt good when there was nothing to capture. Leveraging the self-revealing experience, UG-4 improved their meal decisions: *“if I logged something that seemed unhealthy then I would try to balance that out.”*  
与 HG 参与者相比，UG 参与者更惊讶地发现自己的饮食相当不健康（UG-6，UG-7）。例如，UG-7 指出 *：“我喜欢把自己看作一个健康饮食者\[...\]但我意识到，很多我偶尔以为自己有的东西，其实比我想象的要不健康得多。我想是有意识地思考他们，并且必须记录一切，真的让我意识到了这一点。”* UG-6 还表示：“ *我想我从没意识到自己吃了多少奶酪。我发现我* 吃什么都多了很多脂肪。”本科参与者（UG-3、UG-5、UG-7）也表达了内疚感，但与 HG 参与者相反;他们在捕捉到不健康的食物时感到内疚，当没有可捕捉的时则感到愉悦。利用自我揭示的体验，UG-4 改善了他们的饮食决策：“ *如果我记录了看起来不健康的东西，我会尽量平衡。”*

Participants in both groups found the weekly summary helpful, especially for reflecting on what they had eaten over the previous weeks and the nutritional facts. With the weekly summary, participants gained nutrition knowledge: *“ I could definitely predict what nutrition facts within a food after the study.”* (UG-10), and became more informed about what to eat: *“I am more aware what I should be or should not be eating every day.”* (H-8). But many were not sure how to interpret and make use of this information: *“whats the difference between calorie and carbohydrate?”* (HG-13), *“ I dont know what to do with it”* (HG-3). Two participants (HG-5, HG-10) indicated their doubt on the accuracy of the nutrition facts: *“but you don’t have the size \[offood\]. The only thing you have is my finger”* (HG-5).  
两组参与者都觉得每周总结很有帮助，尤其是回顾过去几周的饮食和营养成分表。通过每周总结，参与者获得了营养知识：“ *研究结束后，我确实能预测食物中的营养成分。”* （UG-10），并且对该吃什么有了更清晰的认识：“ *我更清楚每天应该吃什么或不应该吃什么。”* （H-8）。但许多人不确定如何解读和利用这些信息：“ *卡路里和碳水化合物有什么区别？”* （HG-13）， *“我不知道该怎么用它”* （HG-3）。两位参与者（HG-5，HG-10）对营养成分的准确性表示怀疑：“ *但你没有食物的大小。你唯一拥有的就是我的手指“* （HG-5）。

### Effect on Food Choice Intention对食物选择意图的影响

Thinking about whether meals were healthy or unhealthy made some of the participants become more cautious about their food choices. We found that tracking healthy meals created an overt positive influence for most HG participants. Interestingly, having to capture at least one healthy meal was mentioned frequently by HG participants during the interview: *“It kind of helped me target \[a healthy meal\] for at least one of the meals of my day, \[… \] It helped me to push myself to eat a little bit healthier”* (HG-4). Particularly, participants who already considered themselves *“healthy eaters”* thought tracking healthy meals was a *“good opportunity to keep up with healthy food”* (HG-12). Participants with diet-related health concerns showed a personal interest in making better meal choices (HG-10). Through tracking healthy meals, HG participants expressed that they wanted to be healthier, and acted on these thoughts by avoiding days with nothing healthy to upload, a way of motivating their healthy eating behaviors (HG-1, HG-13).  
思考餐食是否健康，使一些参与者对自己的食物选择变得更加谨慎。我们发现，追踪健康饮食对大多数 HG 参与者产生了明显的积极影响。有趣的是，HG 参与者在采访中经常提到必须捕捉至少一顿健康餐：“ *这多少帮助我为一天中至少一顿饭设定了\[健康餐食\]，\[...\]它帮助我推动自己吃得更健康一些“* （HG-4）。特别是那些已经认为 *自己是“健康饮食者”* 的参与者，认为追踪健康餐食是 *“保持健康饮食的好机会”* （HG-12）。有饮食相关健康问题的参与者表现出对改善饮食选择的个人兴趣（HG-10）。通过追踪健康饮食，HG 参与者表达了他们想要变得更健康，并通过避免没有健康食物可上传的日子来行动，以激励他们的健康饮食行为（HG-1，HG-13）。

On the other hand, for UG participants, tracking unhealthy meals had a cautionary effect *prior* to eating something unhealthy (UG-1,4,5,6,7). It made them judge their food a more critically (UG-6); similarly, UG-1 recalled, *“\[I\] thought, ‘is this a healthy meal or unhealthy meal?’ And then I actually tried to avoid eating what I considered unhealthy.”* The action of logging meals made participants pass pre-judgments on their meal decisions, and once something was recognized as unhealthy, it deterred them from eating it (UG-5).  
另一方面，对于本科参与者来说，在食用不健康食物 *前* 记录不健康饮食具有警示作用（UG-1,4,5,6,7）。这让他们对食物的评判更加严格（UG-6）;同样，UG-1 回忆 *道：“我当时想，'这是一顿健康的饭还是不健康的饭？'然后我实际上试图避免吃我认为不健康的东西。”* 记录餐食的行为使参与者对他们的用餐决策产生先入为主的判断，一旦某样东西被认定为不健康，就会阻止他们食用（UG-5）。

## Discussion 讨论

### Valence of Meal Tracking餐食追踪的价数

Many existing food tracking tools are similarly designed in terms of target behavior and data type, focusing on capturing food items, calories, and amounts in an exact manner. Also, they expect the users to track as many data as possible, with an assumption that this approach will give a complete understanding of one’s diet. However, there are two problems with this approach. First, tracking every food item might not be necessary for everyone, depending on his or her goal of food tracking [^24]. Second, existing food tracking tools often lack support for reflection, which is a huge missed opportunity because people learn from reflecting on data, not from the data themselves. Thus, we argue that designers need to attend to ways in which self-monitoring tools can afford self-reflection.  
许多现有的食物追踪工具在目标行为和数据类型方面也同样设计，专注于精确捕捉食物、卡路里和数量。此外，他们期望用户尽可能多地跟踪数据，并假设这种方法能全面了解个人的饮食情况。然而，这种做法存在两个问题。首先，追踪每一项食物可能并非每个人都必须，这取决于他们对食物追踪 [^24] 的目标。其次，现有的食物追踪工具往往缺乏反思支持，这是一个巨大的错失机会，因为人们是通过反思数据来学习，而不是从数据本身学习。因此，我们认为设计师需要关注自我监控工具能够实现自我反思的方式。

This work sheds light on how “valence of meals” could be used in promoting reflective meal tracking experience. Tracking only healthy or unhealthy meals, as opposed to tracking all food items, could reduce tracking burden and promote people’s reflection on their meals and eating habits by having them pay more attention to their own food practice. For example, we observed that participants from two groups could assess a similar meal differently, as a result of focusing on healthy or unhealthy aspects of the meal. Reflecting on the valence of their previous food choices, participants also thought about their food choice for the future (e.g., “What healthy item should I have?”)  
这项工作揭示了“餐食价值”如何被用来促进反思性饮食追踪体验。仅追踪健康或不健康餐食，而非追踪所有食物，可以减轻追踪负担，并通过更多关注自身饮食习惯，促进人们对饮食和饮食习惯的反思。例如，我们观察到两组参与者可能因关注餐食的健康或不健康方面而对类似餐食有不同评价。回顾过去食物选择的价值，参与者还思考了未来的饮食选择（例如，“我应该吃什么健康食品？”）

We found no statistically significant difference between the HG and UG groups in terms of tracking behaviors and changes in dietary quality score, which might be due to our small sample size. However, our qualitative findings from participants’ written notes and interviews provided rich insights on how the valence of meal tracking affected participants’ assessment on meal healthiness, self-reflection, and food choice intention differently. In our study, tracking healthy meals had many positive outcomes for the HG participants. It strengthened HG participants’ intention to maintain their healthy eating habits (e.g., capture at least one healthy item). Tracking healthy meals also provided HG participants with a satisfactory view of their meals, and of themselves in return. However, it may leave less room to be self-critical, taking away opportunities to improve their current behavior. Given that behavior change is one of the important goals of self-monitoring [^45], tracking healthy meals only, or more broadly, tracking only the positive side of behaviors could be ineffective for those who want to identify shortcomings and nuanced problems. Our HG participants then turned to the frequency of the healthy meals, or lack thereof, to verify whether they were on the right track. This approach could give some clues about one’s dietary problems, but only at a superficial level. On the other hand, tracking unhealthy meals had both positive and negative effects. Although some UG participants were confronted with an unsatisfactory view of themselves, having to capture unhealthy items prompted them to reflect on their meals and provided opportunities to improve their existing eating habits and mend the negative self-view. Furthermore, tracking unhealthy meals amplified UG participants’ self-reflection on their eating habits, and helped deter them from eating unhealthy food. This observation corresponds to prior research suggesting that monitoring undesirable behavior can effectively reduce its occurrence [^42]. Tracking negative behaviors therefore could be appropriate when the goal is to identify problems, rather than casual tracking.  
我们在追踪行为和饮食质量评分变化方面未发现统计学上显著差异，这可能是由于样本量较小。然而，我们通过参与者的书面笔记和访谈获得的定性发现，提供了丰富的见解，说明了餐食追踪的价值如何以不同方式影响参与者对餐食健康、自我反思和食物选择意愿的评估。在我们的研究中，追踪健康餐食对 HG 参与者带来了许多积极效果。它增强了 HG 参与者保持健康饮食习惯的意愿（例如，至少捕捉一项健康食品）。追踪健康餐食还为 HG 参与者提供了对自己餐食及自身满意的认识。然而，这可能会减少自我批评的空间，剥夺改善当前行为的机会。鉴于行为改变是自我监测 [^45] 的重要目标之一，仅追踪健康饮食，或更广泛地说，仅追踪行为的积极一面，对于那些希望识别缺点和细致问题的人来说可能无效。我们的 HG 参与者随后关注健康餐食的频率或缺乏，以验证自己是否走在正确的道路上。这种方法可能提供一些关于饮食问题的线索，但仅限于表面层面。另一方面，记录不健康饮食既有正面也有负面影响。尽管部分本科参与者面对了对自己不满意的看法，但捕捉不健康食品促使他们反思饮食，并为改善现有饮食习惯和修复负面自我观提供了机会。 此外，追踪不健康饮食增强了本科参与者对自身饮食习惯的自我反思，帮助他们避免摄入不健康的食物。这一观察与先前研究表明，监测不良行为可以有效减少其发生。 [^42] 因此，当目标是识别问题时，追踪负面行为可能是合适的，而不是随意追踪。

Taken together, we suspect that valance choice should be made depending on people’s current states and goals. Tracking healthy meals could be helpful for maintaining healthy eating habits for those who already have healthy diets, while tracking unhealthy meals could be helpful in deterring people from eating unhealthy food, especially when their goal is to change their unhealthy diet. While we do not suggest either one is superior to the other in promoting healthy eating, the results indicate the importance of providing people with a filter—that is, the *valence of behavior* that they can use to judge behaviors when they track data or reflect on the captured data. We encourage other researchers to incorporate and leverage the valence of a tracking item in designing reflective self-tracking systems. For example, we envision a food tracking system that asks people to track either healthy or unhealthy food on alternate weeks, urging participants to reflect on their eating habits from both perspectives. In addition, participants’ diverse diet practices, cultures, preferences, and personalities might have influenced their attitudes toward meal healthiness. Therefore, exploring how individual traits affect people’s response to the valence of tracking warrants future research.  
综合来看，我们怀疑应根据人们当前的状态和目标来选择平衡。追踪健康餐食有助于维持那些已经有健康饮食的人，而追踪不健康餐食则有助于阻止人们食用不健康的食物，尤其是当他们的目标是改变不健康饮食时。虽然我们并不认为两者在促进健康饮食方面更优，但结果表明为人们提供过滤器的重要性——即他们在追踪数据或反思数据时，可以用来判断行为的 *行为* 价值。我们鼓励其他研究者在设计反思自我跟踪系统时，利用追踪项目的价值。例如，我们设想了一个食物追踪系统，要求参与者在每隔一周记录健康或不健康的食物，鼓励参与者从两个角度反思自己的饮食习惯。此外，参与者多样的饮食习惯、文化、偏好和个性也可能影响他们对饮食健康的态度。因此，探索个体性状如何影响人们对追踪价值的反应，是未来研究的必要条件。

### Supporting Reflection Throughout the Process of Self-tracking支持自我追踪过程中的反思

Reflection could happen throughout various steps in self-tracking. One framework we used in designing our study was from Ploderer and colleagues: reflection-in-action through real-time feedback and reflection-on-action through aggregated feedback [^44]. This framework helps designers systematically think about ways to support different types of reflection. However, we learned that this dichotomous view of reflection is well-suited for automated tracking, but less so for manual tracking. In automated tracking, where data are being captured at the time of occurrence of an action (e.g., Fitbit, a real-time step count tracker), it is feasible to create and send real-time feedback. However, in manual tracking—such as the photo-based meal tracking combined with written comments, or traditional manual food logging, there is a time gap between when an action occurs and when the data is being captured (usually at the end of the day). Thus, existing food tracking systems, including our own study design, intervene at the time of data capture, rather than during the eating moment. Similarly, YouAte [^9] asks people to mark whether a meal is on-path with their eating goals at the moment of tracking, and answer a set of simple questions, such as how they feel about having this meal after tracking. Going forward, we see opportunities in promoting reflection-in-action by encouraging reflective activities right before the eating moment—for example, through an in-situ prompts that ask people to answer health-related questions about their upcoming meal choice. Such prompts can leverage location sensing (e.g., restaurant), wearable devices (e.g., detecting eating gestures [^48]), social media activities (e.g., eating-related posts), and personal eating routines (e.g., breakfast, lunch, and dinner time for individuals).  
反思可以在自我追踪的各个步骤中进行。我们在设计研究时采用了 Ploderer 及其同事的框架：通过实时反馈进行反思，以及通过汇总反馈进行行动反思 [^44] 。该框架帮助设计师系统地思考支持不同类型的反思。然而，我们了解到这种二分法的反射观适合自动跟踪，但对手动跟踪则不那么适用。在自动化跟踪中，即在动作发生时采集数据（例如 Fitbit，实时步数追踪器），可以创建并发送实时反馈。然而，在手动跟踪中——如结合照片的餐食跟踪加上书面评论，或传统的人工食物记录，从行动发生到数据被采集之间存在时间间隔（通常是在一天结束时）。因此，现有的食物追踪系统，包括我们自己的研究设计，是在数据采集时介入，而非在用餐时。同样，YouAte [^9] 要求人们在记录时标记一餐是否符合他们的饮食目标，并回答一组简单问题，比如记录后吃这顿饭的感受。展望未来，我们看到通过鼓励在用餐前进行反思活动来促进行动反思的机会——例如，通过现场提示，让人们回答关于即将到来的餐食选择的健康相关问题。 此类提示可以利用位置感知（例如餐厅）、可穿戴设备（例如检测进食手势 [^48] ）、社交媒体活动（例如与饮食相关的帖子）以及个人饮食习惯（例如个人的早餐、午餐和晚餐时间）。

Reflection-on-action happened when participants reviewed the weekly summary, which helped them be aware of their overall eating habits and set goals for upcoming weeks. Although many food tracking systems support reflection-on-action through providing aggregated feedback on food consumption over time (e.g., calorie intake trends), existing feedback (including the feedback we provided in our study) is insufficient due to its interpretation difficulties, missing entries, and limited information for self-discovery. To better support reflection-on-action, we suggest that food tracking systems minimize missing entries by sending in-situ reminders for people to track their meals through detecting eating moments, or by capturing food photos automatically with embedded smart sensors. We also suggest that the aggregated feedback highlight abnormal information such as binge eating to support critical reflection-on-action [^20].  
参与者回顾每周总结时，进行了行动反思，帮助他们了解整体饮食习惯并设定未来几周的目标。尽管许多食品追踪系统通过提供食物消费随时间的汇总反馈（例如热量摄入趋势）支持行动反思，但现有反馈（包括我们研究中提供的反馈）由于解释困难、缺失条目以及有限的自我发现信息，仍不够充分。为了更好地支持行动中的反思，我们建议食物追踪系统通过现场提醒用户通过检测用餐时刻来记录餐食，或通过内置智能传感器自动捕捉食物照片，来减少遗漏条目。我们还建议汇总反馈突出异常信息，如暴食，以支持批判性反思。 [^20]

## Conclusion 结论

To study how the valence of meal tracking affects people’s food tracking experiences, we created two conditions-one focusing on capturing healthy meals, the other capturing unhealth meals-and conducted a four-week diary study with 22 college students. We found that participants in both groups became more aware of their diet, but the valence of meal affected the two groups differently on their meal assessment, self-reflection, and food choice intention. Particularly, tracking healthy meals helped participants reflect on their diet positively, and encouraged them to maintain healthy eating habits, although it did not leave enough room for “critical” self-reflection. Tracking unhealthy meals deterred participants from eating unhealthy foods and nudged them to form an unsatisfactory self-view. Our study showed that implementing a particular valence of a target behavior in designing self-tracking systems could provide opportunities for self-reflections. In closing, we argue that *valence* is an important concept to incorporate in designing reflective self-tracking systems, and call for more work leveraging this concept in the future.  
为了研究餐食追踪的价值如何影响人们的食物追踪体验，我们设计了两个条件——一个专注于捕捉健康餐食，另一个用于捕捉不健康的餐食——并对 22 名大学生进行了为期四周的日记研究。我们发现两组参与者对自身饮食的意识有所提升，但餐食的价值对两组的餐食评估、自我反思和食物选择意图的影响不同。特别是，追踪健康餐食帮助参与者积极反思自己的饮食，并鼓励他们保持健康的饮食习惯，尽管这并没有留下足够的空间进行“批判性”的自我反思。追踪不健康饮食阻止参与者食用不健康食物，并促使他们形成不满意的自我观。我们的研究表明，在设计自我跟踪系统时，实施特定目标行为的价值，可以为自我反思提供机会。最后，我们认为 *价性* 是设计反射式自我跟踪系统时一个重要的概念，并呼吁未来有更多工作利用这一概念。
