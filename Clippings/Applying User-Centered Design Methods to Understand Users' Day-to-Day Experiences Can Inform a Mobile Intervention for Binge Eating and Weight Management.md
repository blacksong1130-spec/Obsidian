## Abstract 摘要

**Introduction:** Weight loss apps to date have not directly addressed binge eating. To inform the design of a new mobile behavioral intervention that addresses binge eating and weight management, we applied user-centered design methods to qualitatively assess how target intervention consumers experience these conditions in their day-to-day lives.  
**引言：** 到目前为止，减肥应用还没有直接针对暴食症。为了设计一种针对暴食和体重管理的新型移动行为干预，我们采用以用户为中心的设计方法，定性评估目标干预消费者在日常生活中如何体验这些状况。

**Methods:** The participants were 22 adults with self-reported obesity (body mass index ≥30) and recurrent binge eating (≥12 episodes in 3 months) who were interested in losing weight and reducing binge eating. The participants completed a digital diary study, which is a user-centered design technique for capturing individuals' day-to-day experiences in relevant contexts. Qualitative data describing the participants' experiences with binge eating and obesity were analyzed using thematic analysis. The results were then used to create personas (i.e., character archetypes of different intervention consumers).  
**方法：** 参与者为 22 名自报肥胖（身体质量指数≥30）且反复暴食≥（3 个月内 12 次发作）的成年人，他们对减肥和减少暴食有兴趣。参与者完成了一项数字日记研究，这是一种以用户为中心的设计技术，用于捕捉个体在相关情境下的日常经历。通过主题分析分析了描述参与者暴食和肥胖经历的定性数据。结果随后被用来创建角色（即不同干预消费者的角色原型）。

**Results:** The participants described triggers for binge eating and indicated that binge eating and excess weight negatively impact their mental health, physical health, and quality of life. The resulting personas reflected five different struggles individuals with these health problems experience in managing their binge eating and weight.  
**结果：** 参与者描述了暴食的诱因，并指出暴食和过重会对他们的心理健康、身体健康和生活质量产生负面影响。这些人格反映了患有这些健康问题的人们在管理暴食和体重方面遇到的五种不同困难。

**Conclusions:** Individuals with binge eating and obesity have varying precipitants of problematic eating as well as varying motivations for and challenges to behavior change. To meet the needs of all who seek intervention, an ideal intervention design will account for variations in these factors and be relevant to diverse experiences. Insights from the diary study and resulting personas will inform the next phases of the user-centered design process of iteratively designing prototypes and testing the intervention in practice.  
**结论：** 暴食和肥胖患者有不同的诱因，以及对行为改变的动机和挑战。为了满足所有寻求干预者的需求，理想的干预设计应考虑这些因素的差异，并与多样化的经历相关。日记研究及其所得人物形象的洞见将为以用户为中心的设计流程的下一阶段提供参考，即迭代设计原型并测试干预措施。

## Introduction 简介

Up to 30% of treatment-seeking individuals with obesity engage in binge eating ([^2] – [^4]), and the majority (>75%) of people with recurrent binge eating also have overweight or obesity ([^5]). We are designing a new mobile behavioral intervention for people with obesity and recurrent binge eating who want to lose weight and reduce their binge eating, and intervention targets will be drawn from evidence-based psychological and behavioral principles for managing weight and binge eating. An intervention for obesity and binge eating is needed because the psychological or behavioral interventions that have been tested to date have not produced substantive changes in both of these outcomes ([^6], [^7]). Moreover, to our knowledge, weight loss apps have not directly addressed binge eating. This is problematic because, without adequate attention to the mechanisms that drive and maintain binge eating, some behavioral recommendations for weight loss (e.g., dietary restriction) can exacerbate eating disorder behaviors. Therefore, specialized intervention that concurrently addresses obesity and binge eating is warranted.  
多达 30%的寻求治疗的肥胖患者会暴食（ [^2] – [^4] ），而大多数反复暴食者（>75%）同时也超重或肥胖（ [^5] ）。我们正在为肥胖和反复暴食、希望减肥和减少暴食的人群设计一种新的移动行为干预措施，干预目标将基于循证的心理和行为原则来管理体重和暴食。需要针对肥胖和暴食的干预措施，因为迄今为止测试的心理或行为干预措施未能在这两个结局上产生实质性改变（ [^6] ， [^7] ）。此外，据我们所知，减肥应用并未直接解决暴食问题。这很有问题，因为如果不充分关注驱动和维持暴食的机制，某些减肥行为建议（例如饮食限制）可能会加重饮食失调行为。因此，需要同时针对肥胖和暴食的专业干预措施。

Digital interventions offer an exciting opportunity to increase access to care and extend the reach of services to individuals when they are needed most ([^8]). We are using a mobile format so that our new intervention can scale to the substantial number of individuals in need of services ([^3], [^5]). However, to ensure that our intervention will be used in the moments and contexts when it is needed most, it must be designed to reflect the needs of individuals with obesity and binge eating and address the diversity of experiences these individuals face in trying to manage their eating and weight. Too often, digital health interventions have been designed based on what clinicians think intervention consumers need without sufficiently accounting for what they want ([^9], [^10]). This approach has been problematic as consumers do not use the interventions ([^11]), and consistent examples also indicate challenges with their successful implementation ([^10]). To rectify these problems, clinical scientists need innovative methodologies for designing digital interventions that achieve high engagement and clinical significance.  
数字干预为增加护理可及性提供了令人振奋的机会，并在个人最需要时扩大服务覆盖范围（ [^8] ）。我们采用移动形式，以便新干预能够覆盖大量需要服务的人群（ [^3] ， [^5] ）。然而，为了确保我们的干预措施能在最需要的时刻和情境中发挥作用，必须设计以反映肥胖和暴食个体的需求，并关注这些人在管理饮食和体重时所面临的多样化体验。数字健康干预往往基于临床医生认为干预消费者的需求设计，却未充分考虑他们的需求（ [^9] ， [^10] ）。这种方法存在问题，因为消费者并未使用这些干预措施（ [^11] ），而且持续的例子也显示了其成功实施的挑战（ [^10] ）。为了解决这些问题，临床科学家需要创新的方法来设计数字干预措施，以实现高度的参与度和临床意义。

We are taking a user-centered design approach to create this new intervention. User-centered design is a methodology that focuses on engaging deeply with end-users (i.e., target users of an intervention) to create technological solutions that are grounded in information about the individuals who will be using them and the settings in which they will be deployed ([^12] – [^14]). By putting end-users at the center of the design process, this methodology results in technologies that are easier to understand and use ([^12] – [^14]). From a clinical standpoint, our new mobile intervention is being designed to address five putative intervention targets that are theorized as mechanisms that contribute to a cycle of binge eating and changes in weight: overvaluation of weight and shape, unhealthy weight control practices, negative affect, dietary intake, and physical activity. To address these targets, the intervention will draw on evidence-based principles from behavioral weight loss treatment and cognitive behavioral therapy for binge eating ([^15] – [^16]). However, *how* the intervention is designed to address these intervention targets, so that it is effectively delivered in a mobile format, requires collaboratively involving end-users in the design process.  
我们采用以用户为中心的设计方法来创建这一新干预措施。以用户为中心的设计是一种方法论，重点在于深度与终端用户（即干预的目标用户）互动，以创建基于使用者及其部署环境信息的技术解决方案（ [^12] – [^14] ）。通过将终端用户置于设计过程的核心，该方法论使技术更易于理解和使用（ [^12] – [^14] ）。从临床角度来看，我们的新型移动干预旨在针对五个假设的干预目标，这些目标被认为是导致暴饮暴食和体重变化循环的机制：体重和体型的高估、不健康的体重控制实践、负面情绪、饮食摄入和身体活动。为实现这些目标，干预措施将借鉴行为减重治疗和认知行为疗法的循证原则（ [^15] – [^16] ）。然而， *如何* 设计干预以实现这些干预目标，并以移动形式有效实施，需要最终用户协作参与设计过程。

The first step in the user-centered design process is to conduct a needs assessment. A needs assessment is an essential aspect of design. By investigating the needs, goals, and preferences of end-users, we can ensure that a technological solution is designed to address a problem or fill an unmet need. Despite a robust literature documenting problems associated with binge eating and weight \[e.g., ([^7], [^17])\] and the types of behavioral and cognitive strategies that would likely lead to improvement ([^15] – [^16]), we need a better understanding of how individuals with obesity and binge eating experience these problems in their day-to-day lives. Deeply understanding individuals' experiences—and the motivations and barriers that they face in managing these health problems—can inform how a mobile intervention could be designed to fit into these individuals' daily routines.  
以用户为中心的设计流程的第一步是进行需求评估。需求评估是设计中至关重要的方面。通过调查终端用户的需求、目标和偏好，我们可以确保技术解决方案能够解决问题或满足未被满足的需求。尽管有大量文献记录了暴食和体重相关的问题\[例如，（ [^7] ， [^17] ）\] 以及可能带来改善的行为和认知策略类型（ [^15] – [^16] ），但我们仍需更好地理解肥胖和暴食者在日常生活中如何经历这些问题。深入了解个人的经历——以及他们在管理这些健康问题时面临的动机和障碍——可以指导移动干预如何设计以适应他们的日常生活。

A variety of methods can be used to conduct a needs assessment, such as interviews, focus groups, and observations ([^13]). To design our intervention, we used a method called a digital diary study, the purpose of which is to learn about and understand people's experiences as they occur in the context of their day-to-day lives. A diary study entails prompting users at various moments to share details about a particular experience. The digital format means that the users submit their entries *via* an online/mobile platform (as will be described in the “METHODS”). Various response formats can be administered in a diary study to collect qualitative and/or quantitative data (e.g., video recordings, uploading photographs, open-ended text responses, multiple choice, and yes/no questions). This study analyzed qualitative data from video recordings and open-ended text responses.  
需求评估可采用多种方法，如访谈、焦点小组和观察（ [^13] ）。为了设计我们的干预措施，我们采用了一种称为数字日记研究的方法，其目的是了解和理解人们在日常生活中经历的过程。日记研究是指在不同时间点引导用户分享关于某次经历的细节。数字格式意味着用户 *通过* 在线/移动平台提交作品（详见“方法”部分）。日记研究中可采用多种回答格式以收集定性和/或定量数据（如视频录制、上传照片、开放式文本回答、选择题和是非题）。本研究分析了视频录制和开放式文本回答的定性数据。

Data from a needs assessment are then used to inform the next step in the user-centered design process: ideation. Ideation is the brainstorming phase, when ideas and concepts for the intervention are generated. Brainstormed ideas are then used to create initial intervention designs. One method used in the ideation phase that can help inform intervention ideas is creating personas. Personas are composite archetypes of different potential intervention users, presented as distinct individual users, that show the diversity of perspectives that need to be considered in an intervention design ([^18], [^19]). Personas represent broad segments of actual users through detailed descriptions of hypothetical users. Created based on aggregated data from multiple potential users ([^20], [^21]), such as captured through a needs assessment, personas are presented as individual people and convey specific details about them, such as personal characteristics, motivations and goals, needs, and expectations. Personas help designers move from understanding users' needs to brainstorming and designing solutions that meet these needs. The value of personas is in serving as a reference point that designers can prospectively use to inform, justify, and build consensus around design decisions as well as assess that iterative designs reflect their intended users ([^18]). Although creating personas cannot ensure that the eventual intervention yields high engagement, the use of personas helps ensure that interventions respond to the diversity of experiences and needs within intended user populations ([^22]), which, in turn, may lead to heightened engagement.  
需求评估数据随后被用来指导以用户为中心设计流程的下一步：构思。创意阶段是头脑风暴阶段，在此阶段产生干预的理念和想法。随后，头脑风暴的想法被用来设计初步干预方案。在创意阶段，一种有助于指导干预想法的方法就是创建角色扮演。Persona 是不同潜在干预使用者的复合原型，呈现为不同的个体用户，展示了干预设计中需要考虑的多元视角（ [^18] ， [^19] ）。Persona 通过对假设用户的详细描述，代表了实际用户的广泛群体。基于多个潜在用户的汇总数据（ [^20] ， [^21] ），例如通过需求评估收集的资料，人物扮演以个体形式呈现，传达其具体细节，如个人特征、动机和目标、需求及期望。Persona 帮助设计师从理解用户需求转向头脑风暴和设计满足这些需求的解决方案。人物角色的价值在于作为参考点，设计师可以前瞻性地参考、证明合理性并围绕设计决策建立共识，同时评估迭代设计是否反映其预期用户（ [^18] ）。虽然创建角色无法保证最终干预带来高度参与度，但角色的使用有助于确保干预措施响应目标用户群体内多样化的体验和需求（ [^22] ），进而可能带来更高的参与度。

Given these advantages, persona use is growing within behavioral health \[e.g., ([^23] – [^24])\]. For example, personas have been developed to reflect hypothetical users of a weight loss coaching app ([^25]). In that study, five personas were created, which reflected nuanced descriptions of different end-users' motivations to change their behaviors, the struggles they face in the process of losing weight, and the perceptions they have about their behavior and weight. Each persona described the hypothetical user's approach and readiness to engage in behavior change around losing weight, with a focus on their level of autonomy to pursue behavioral changes. The authors concluded that creating the personas enabled their designers to empathize with the struggles that individuals face in changing their behavior and to understand the variety of factors that must be addressed in a coaching app to help move people toward changing their behavior.  
鉴于这些优势，人格使用在行为健康领域正在增长\[例如，（ [^23] – [^24] ）\]。例如，角色角色已被开发出来反映减肥教练应用的假设用户（ [^25] ）。在这项研究中，创建了五个角色，反映了不同终端用户改变行为动机的细致描述、他们在减肥过程中面临的困难，以及他们对自身行为和体重的看法。每个人格描述了假设用户在减肥过程中采取的态度和准备度，重点关注其追求行为改变的自主性水平。作者总结道，创建这些角色让设计师能够同理个体在改变行为过程中所面临的困难，并理解在教练应用中必须解决的各种因素，以帮助人们推动行为改变。

Despite the benefits of personas to the process of designing and validating a technology, to our knowledge, personas have not been applied to individuals struggling with binge eating and seeking weight loss nor to binge eating in general. Thus, the purpose of this paper is to (a) disseminate results from a digital diary study needs assessment with end-users that aimed to answer the research question of how individuals with obesity and binge eating experience these problems in their day-to-day lives and (b) present five personas that were created based on the insights that emerged from data analysis. Design methods are being increasingly used for health-related interventions \[e.g., ([^25] – [^26])\], though they have been largely underutilized in the clinical science field ([^27]) and, to our knowledge, rarely represented in eating disorders \[cf ([^28], [^29])\]. Therefore, this paper offers an important example of the application of user-centered design methods to mobile behavioral intervention design. This work has relevance for informing future approaches to designing digital mental and behavioral health interventions.  
尽管 Persona 对设计和验证技术过程有好处，但据我们所知，Persona 尚未应用于与暴食和减肥作斗争的个体，也未适用于暴食症。因此，本文的目的是：（a）向终端用户传播数字日记研究需求评估的结果，旨在回答肥胖和暴食个体在日常生活中如何经历这些问题的研究问题;（b）呈现基于数据分析洞察创建的五个角色扮演。设计方法正日益被用于健康相关干预\[例如，（ [^25] – [^26] ）\]，尽管在临床科学领域大多未被充分利用（ [^27] ），据我们所知，在饮食障碍中也很少见\[参见（ [^28] ， [^29] ）\]。因此，本文为以用户为中心的设计方法在移动行为干预设计中的应用提供了重要范例。这项工作对于未来设计数字心理和行为健康干预方法具有重要意义。

## Methods 方法

### Participants 参赛者

The participants were non-pregnant, English-speaking adults with obesity \[body mass index (BMI) ≥30 based on self-reported height and weight\] and self-reported recurrent binge eating (≥12 binge eating episodes in 3 months). Binge eating was defined as “when someone eats an unusually large amount of food and feels a sense of loss of control while eating” ([^30]). The eligible individuals were also interested in losing weight and reducing their binge eating. Among those eligible to participate, the final sample was selected to ensure a diverse cohort of target intervention users based on race/ethnicity, sex, and age. Specifically, the participants were purposely selected across a range of ages and, for race/ethnicity, in proportions approximate to the racial/ethnic distribution of individuals in Chicago according to 2010 census data ([^31]) where future end-users were planned to be drawn for efficacy testing. More females than males were selected because the prevalence of binge eating is higher in females than in males ([^32]).  
参与者为非孕、讲英语的成年人，具有肥胖\[BMI3≥0，基于自我报告的身高和体重\]和自报反复暴食（≥3 个月内 12 次暴食发作）。暴食被定义为“当某人吃了异常大量的食物，并在进食时感到失控”（ 页）。符合条件的人群也希望减肥并减少暴饮暴食。在符合条件的参与者中，最终样本被选定为基于种族/族裔、性别和年龄的多样化目标干预用户群体。具体来说，参与者被有意地从不同年龄层选拔，并且根据 2010 年人口普查数据（36）中，针对芝加哥个人的种族/族裔分布比例（ [^31] ），计划抽选未来最终用户进行疗效测试。女性被选中人数多于男性，因为女性暴食的发生率高于男性（ [^32] ）。

Twenty-five individuals were invited to participate, of whom 22 completed the study and were included in these analyses. Though small for clinical research, this sample size was anticipated to be sufficient to achieve saturation ([^33]) and is consistent with research in the field of human–computer interaction ([^34]).  
共有 25 人被邀请参与，其中 22 人完成了研究并被纳入分析。虽然对临床研究来说样本量较小，但预期样本量足以实现饱和（ [^33] ），并且与人机交互领域的研究结果一致（ [^34] ）。

### Procedure 程序

The study procedures occurred entirely online using dscout, a digital platform for qualitative and market research that enables capturing of the participants' in-the-moment, in-context experiences over time ([^35]). Interested individuals responded to a study advertisement and completed a screener in dscout to assess eligibility. The invited participants then completed a digital diary study over 1 month that focused on understanding how binge eating and weight impact the participants' day-to-day lives, factors that trigger binge eating, who and what motivates them to work on their binge eating and weight, and the strategies that they use in their daily lives to manage their binge eating and weight.  
研究过程完全在线完成，使用 dscout，这是一个数字平台，用于定性和市场调研，能够记录参与者当下、置于上下文中的体验（ [^35] ）。有意者响应了研究广告，并在 dscout 中完成筛查以评估资格。受邀参与者随后完成了一个月的数字日记研究，重点了解暴食和体重如何影响参与者的日常生活、触发暴食的因素、谁和什么激励他们改善暴食和体重，以及他们在日常生活中管理暴食和体重的策略。

The study was administered over nine parts. The participants submitted entries in response to each part, and the number of entries requested for each part varied depending on the research questions entailed (range = 1–4 entries per part; total entries = 14). For each part, the participants were asked to submit their entry or entries at any time of their choosing within the requested time window for that part (range = 2–7 days). Entries could be submitted from the participants' smartphones, and each entry took only a few minutes to complete. The participants were sent reminders through the platform to complete the entries before the scheduled end-date for each part. Parts were sequentially made available so that the participants focused on only one at a time.  
该研究分为九个部分进行。参与者对每个部分提交了作品，每个部分的投稿数量根据研究问题的不同而异（范围 = 每部分1–4条;总参赛数量=14条）。对于每个部分，参与者被要求在该部分要求的时间窗口内随时提交作品（范围=2–7天）。参赛者可以通过智能手机提交作品，每条作品只需几分钟即可完成。参与者通过平台收到提醒，要求他们在每个部分的预定截止日期前完成参赛作品。部分按顺序提供，使参与者一次只专注于其中一项。

Seven of the nine parts contained video entries (one per part) and open-ended text responses (range = 1–4 per part). For example, in the first part, the participants were asked to record a video while answering the questions “How do binge eating and weight impact your day-to-day life? Do you see these two things as related to each other, and if so, how?” The complete assessment guide is available upon reasonable request from the corresponding author.  
九部分中有七部分包含视频条目（每部分一条）和开放式文本回答（范围=每部分1–4段）。例如，在第一部分中，参与者被要求录制视频，同时回答“暴食和体重如何影响你的日常生活？”的问题。你认为这两者有关联吗？如果有，是怎么关联的？”完整的评估指南可由通讯作者合理请求提供。

The participants who completed all study procedures were compensated $100; all 22 participants included in this analysis were compensated. This study was approved by the Northwestern University Institutional Review Board, and all participants provided online informed consent.  
完成所有研究程序的参与者获得100美元赔偿;所有纳入本分析的22名参与者均获得补偿。本研究已获得西北大学机构审查委员会批准，所有参与者均提供了在线知情同意。

### Analyses 分析

To answer the research question of how individuals with obesity and binge eating experience these problems in their day-to-day lives, the participants' video recordings and open-ended text responses were analyzed. Video recordings were automatically transcribed by the dscout platform and edited for accuracy and deidentification by a study team member. Thematic analysis was used to analyze the data, following Braun and Clark's methodology ([^36]). This involved becoming familiar with the data by reviewing the transcripts, using open coding to create codes, applying the codes to the transcripts, and finally organizing the codes into broader themes. Data were analyzed by two raters, one of whom was a licensed clinical psychologist with expertise in treating binge eating and obesity, and the other was an undergraduate student on the pre-medical track, majoring in biology and Spanish. After independently reviewing the data, the raters each identified a set of codes and then met together to discuss the codes and reach consensus on a codebook. The raters then reviewed the transcripts independently and met frequently to resolve questions about coding *via* consensus and to refine the codebook as needed.  
为了回答肥胖和暴食者如何在日常生活中体验这些问题，研究人员分析了他们的视频录制和开放式文本回答。视频录制由 dscout 平台自动转录，并由研究团队成员进行编辑以保证准确性和识别。采用主题分析方法分析数据，遵循布劳恩和克拉克的方法论（ [^36] 页）。这包括通过审查转录稿来熟悉数据，使用开放编码创建代码，将代码应用于转录，最终将代码组织成更广泛的主题。数据由两名评审员分析，其中一位是拥有治疗暴食和肥胖的执照临床心理学家，另一位是医学预科本科生，主修生物学和西班牙语。在独立审查数据后，评审员各自确定了一组代码，然后聚在一起讨论代码并就密码本达成共识。评审员随后独立审查文字记录，并频繁聚会， *通过共识* 解决编码问题，并根据需要完善编码手册。

After the data were analyzed, personas were created. This process began by identifying commonalities across participants' experiences that demonstrated representative struggles people had in managing their binge eating and weight. More specifically, by gathering a detailed understanding *via* the needs assessment of how individuals experience obesity and binge eating in their day-to-day lives, we learned overarching themes about factors that drive (precipitants) and result from (impacts) binge eating and obesity. Based on these themes, we identified patterns that represented different struggles that individuals experience in managing these problems—which are essential to delineate for understanding how to design a tool that could help someone change their behaviors. These patterns became our five personas. As details for the personas were created, the two raters revisited the transcripts to check and ensure that details about each persona were based on the participants' data from the diary study and that the five personas were comprehensive representations of the data.  
数据分析完成后，角色被创建。这一过程首先识别参与者经历中的共性，这些共同点展示了人们在管理暴食和体重方面所面临的代表性困难。更具体地说，通过需求评估，我们深入了解个体在日常生活中如何体验肥胖和暴食，了解了驱动（诱发因素）和影响暴食和肥胖的因素的总体主题。基于这些主题，我们识别出代表个体在管理这些问题时所经历的不同挣扎的模式——这些模式对于理解如何设计帮助某人改变行为的工具至关重要。这些模式成为了我们的五个人格。在角色细节创建过程中，两位评审员重新审查了文字记录，核对并确保每个角色的细节基于参与者日记研究中的数据，并且五个人角是数据的全面代表。

Consistent with persona design ([^18], [^22], [^37]), each of our personas was designed as a hypothetical user; no persona reflected a single participant in the digital diary study. Although a variety of features can be included in a persona, it was decided that these personas would include a name that encapsulates the key factor for why that hypothetical user struggles with managing their binge eating and weight, a brief description, a quote that demonstrates a statement this hypothetical user would make about their struggles, their motivations for and barriers to changing their behavior around eating and weight management, and an image that captured a precipitating factor associated with binge eating for that hypothetical user.  
与人格设计（ [^18] 、 [^22] 、 [^37] ）一致，我们的每个人格都被设计为假设用户;数字日记研究中没有任何一个参与者的身份。虽然角色可以包含多种特征，但决定这些角色应包含一个名字，概括该假设用户为何难以管理暴食和体重的关键因素，简要描述，一段展示该虚拟用户对其困境的陈述的引言。 他们改变饮食和体重管理行为的动机和障碍，以及一张捕捉该假设用户暴食诱因的图片。

## Results 选举结果

The 22 participants self-identified as primarily female (64%), with a mean age of 37.0 (SD = 10.2) years. The participants self-identified themselves as per the following race/ethnicities: 32% non-Hispanic White, 27% Black, 27% Hispanic, 9% Asian/Pacific Islander, and 5% unknown. At the start of the study, the participants reported an average BMI of 37.1 (SD = 5.4) and having engaged in an average of 20.5 (SD = 7.3) binge episodes over the previous 3 months. All the participants reported prior attempts to lose weight, and 91% reported prior attempts to stop binge eating.  
22 名参与者自认主要为女性（64%），平均年龄为 37.0 岁（标准差=10.2）。参与者自我认同为以下种族/族裔：32%非西班牙裔白人，27%黑人，27%西班牙裔，9%亚裔/太平洋岛民，5%未知。研究开始时，参与者报告的平均 BMI 为 37.1（标准差=5.4），过去三个月平均经历了 20.5 次暴食（标准差=7.3 次）。所有参与者都报告过之前减肥尝试，91%的人曾尝试停止暴食。

### Precipitants of Binge Eating and Obesity暴食和肥胖的诱因

The participants described a multitude of factors that precipitated binge eating, which were grouped into two themes: internal (psychological and physical health) states and situational factors.  
参与者描述了导致暴食的众多因素，这些因素被分为两个主题：内在（心理和身体健康）状态和情境因素。

#### Internal States 内部国家

The most common internal trigger for binge eating was emotions, such as anxiety, stress, and low mood. Boredom also created problems for the participants, as they stated that this led to binge eating or eating unhealthy foods:  
暴食最常见的内在触发因素是情绪，如焦虑、压力和情绪低落。无聊也给参与者带来了问题，他们表示这导致暴食或摄入不健康的食物：

> “When I get bored, sometimes I crave snacks. Instead of having a healthy snack, I eat unhealthy.” \[Participant (Pt) 19\]  
> “我无聊的时候，有时候会想吃点零食。我不吃健康的零食，反而吃得不健康。”\[参与者（第19部分）\]

Physical health problems and sleep issues contributed to binge eating and unhealthy eating. One participant cited lack of sleep as problematic for making healthy decisions:  
身体健康问题和睡眠问题导致了暴食和不健康饮食。一位参与者提到睡眠不足对做出健康决策有影响：

> “Lack of sleep and anxiety are definite triggers. Not getting enough sleep makes making smart decisions so much harder.” (Pt 5)  
> “睡眠不足和焦虑确实是诱因。睡眠不足让做出明智决定变得更加困难。”（第5部分）

Pre-occupation with food or hunger was also problematic. In these instances, the participants described feeling very hungry and feeling unable to think about things besides eating, which could result in binge episodes:  
对食物或饥饿的过度关注也是一个问题。在这些情况下，参与者描述了感到非常饥饿，无法思考除吃东西以外的事情，这可能导致暴食发作：

> “I really want to eat something, and I'm really hungry. \[…\] When you're really thinking about food, everything is food.” (Pt 14)  
> “我真的很想吃点东西，而且我真的很饿。\[…\]当你真正思考食物时，一切都是食物。”（第14部分）

The participants described a perceived need to eat food that was available, regardless of their hunger or interest in the food. In these instances, they felt unable to avoid binge eating:  
参与者描述了无论饥饿或对食物兴趣如何，都感受到必须吃现有食物的需求。在这些情况下，他们觉得无法避免暴饮暴食：

> “Even though the foods were ‘healthy,’ I kept asking myself ‘why?’ I KNEW I wasn't hungry, but just the thought of a tuna sandwich and a bag of chips after the banana was too much to resist.” (Pt 22)  
> “虽然食物'健康'，我还是不断问自己'为什么？'我知道我不饿，但光是想到香蕉之后还能吃金枪鱼三明治和一包薯片，就让我无法抗拒。”（第22部分）

In fact, some participants anticipated and therefore planned for a binge episode:  
事实上，有些参与者预料并因此计划了一次暴食发作：

> “I hate to say it and admit it, but I'm getting ready to do some bingeing. Because I'm in whole foods, I'm getting ready to be at the dessert counter, and just, I'm just buying stuff.” (Pt 9)  
> “我不想承认，但我正准备狂看。因为我在 Whole Foods，我正准备去甜点柜台，然后，我就是在买东西。”（第 9 部分）

Such experiences were also related to difficulties with setting or maintaining goals for healthy eating, which could result in problems with motivation that led to binge eating. For example, a participant described that when they failed to achieve certain health-related goals, they would abandon their plans and engage in unhealthy or binge eating:  
这些经历还与设定或维持健康饮食目标的困难有关，这可能导致动力不足，进而导致暴饮暴食。例如，一位参与者描述了当他们未能实现某些健康相关目标时，他们会放弃计划，进行不健康或暴饮暴食：

> “It (binge eating) started with creating an expectation for myself that I wasn't able to achieve. When I wasn't seeing the results I wanted, I would develop an ‘oh well, it doesn’t even matter' mentality.” (Pt 13)  
> “暴饮暴食始于给自己设定了一个我无法实现的期望。当我看不到想要的结果时，我会养成'算了，这都无所谓'的心态。”（第13部分）

The results in this theme together indicate that it will be important for an intervention to help individuals identify unhelpful patterns of thinking, address negative affect, and learn strategies to change their behaviors in response to these triggers.  
该主题的结果共同表明，干预措施应帮助个体识别无益的思维模式，应对负面情绪，并学习改变行为的策略以应对这些触发因素。

#### Situational Factors 情境因素

Triggers were also commonly driven by situational factors. Situational triggers included factors such as eating alone, engaging in eating-related activities (e.g., events involving food), having access to certain foods, and experiencing interpersonal problems. Late night binge eating was common for the participants. The participants also reported that problematic eating was exacerbated by an unstructured eating pattern. For example, this participant failed to eat much during the day, which led to binge eating that evening:  
触发因素通常也受情境因素驱动。情境触发因素包括独自进食、参与与饮食相关的活动（如与食物有关的事件）、能够接触到特定食物以及经历人际关系问题等因素。参与者常常深夜暴饮暴食。参与者还报告说，非结构化的饮食模式加剧了问题性饮食。例如，这位参与者白天吃得很少，导致当晚暴饮暴食：

> “Binge eating for me is triggered by a necessity to eat late at night and then trying to eat all of my meals at one time, feeling like I missed out on things because I go the whole day without eating.” (Pt 9)  
> “对我来说，暴食是因为必须在深夜吃饭，然后试图一次性吃完所有餐食，感觉自己错过了什么，因为我整天都没吃东西。”（第9部分）

Another example in which unstructured eating created problems was through too much snacking as described by this participant:  
另一个非结构性饮食导致问题的例子是，正如这位参与者所描述的，是通过过量零食：

> “I snack at the weirdest times and throughout the day, primarily eating the wrong snacks.” (Pt 3)  
> “我总是在最奇怪的时间、整天吃零食，主要是吃错零食。”（第三部分）

Eating outside the home, such as eating at fast-food establishments or from a vending machine, was a trigger for unhealthy or binge eating as described by this participant, for whom eating outside the home resulted in secretive eating, which is a hallmark feature of binge eating:  
在户外用餐，比如快餐店或自动售货机，是不健康或暴食的诱因，正如这位参与者所描述的，他们在外吃饭导致了偷偷进食，这是暴食的标志性特征：

> “I'm tired and I just want to stop at the nearest place and eat, and then eat dinner when I get home as well. Because I will make sure to not tell my husband that I stopped.” (Pt 8)  
> “我累了，只想去最近的地方吃点东西，回家后也想吃晚饭。因为我会确保不告诉我丈夫我停了。”（第8部分）

Conversely, some participants described difficulties in sustaining a healthy pattern of eating given a perception that their home environment hindered healthy behaviors. In this example, the participant's partner purchased unhealthy foods, making it harder for the participant to sustain healthy eating:  
相反，一些参与者描述了由于认为家庭环境阻碍健康行为，难以维持健康的饮食模式。在这个例子中，参与者的伴侣购买了不健康的食物，使参与者更难维持健康饮食：

> “I had a miscommunication with my wife. (…) When I was explaining to her we need to get fruit and vegetables, (…) she ended up getting me chicken and cookies instead. And so, I was really trying to get my family to support me with binge eating, but it didn't work.” (Pt 1)  
> “我和我妻子沟通不畅。(…)当我跟她解释我们需要买水果和蔬菜时，（...）她最后给我买了鸡肉和饼干。所以我真的很努力让家人支持我暴饮暴食，但没成功。”（第1部分）

An understanding of precipitants is crucial for informing points for intervention. The results here indicate the powerful role that both internal and external factors can play in binge eating. Thus, an ideal intervention will be designed to account for variation in these factors.  
了解降水因素对于制定干预点至关重要。这里的结果表明，内在和外部因素在暴食中都起着重要作用。因此，理想的干预措施将被设计以考虑这些因素的差异。

### Consequences of Binge Eating and Obesity暴食与肥胖的后果

In understanding how the participants experience binge eating and obesity in their day-to-day lives, themes also emerged on the significant and impairing impact binge eating and obesity have on the participants' physical health, mental health, and quality of life.  
在了解参与者如何在日常生活中体验暴食和肥胖时，还出现了暴食和肥胖对参与者身体健康、心理健康和生活质量的重大且有害影响的主题。

#### Impact on Physical Health对身体健康的影响

The participants commented on the physical health impact of obesity and binge eating, such as feelings of pain or soreness in specific body parts like their knees, hips, and back. Though physical problems were often attributed to excess weight, some participants acknowledged the role of binge eating in causing health problems through weight gain. For example, this participant described how binge eating “too many snacks and junk food” had “impacted my midsection” and caused other health problems:  
参与者评论了肥胖和暴食对身体健康的影响，比如膝盖、臀部和背部等特定部位的疼痛或酸痛感。尽管身体问题常被归因于体重过重，但一些参与者承认暴食在通过体重增加导致健康问题中起到了作用。例如，这位参与者描述了暴食“吃太多零食和垃圾食品”对我的腹部造成了影响“并引发了其他健康问题：

> “I used to just be in such good shape to run around with my kids or play basketball and things. But now I just find myself low energy and oftentimes I have trouble sleeping. And so I just really think the binge eating is affecting my overall health.” (Pt 25)  
> “我以前身体状态很好，可以和孩子们一起跑跑，打篮球什么的。但现在我发现自己精力很低，经常难以入睡。所以我真的觉得暴饮暴食正在影响我的整体健康。”（第25部分）

The participants also described how their weight makes it harder for them to engage in physical activities, like walking, as noted by this participant:  
参与者还描述了他们的体重如何使他们更难参与体育活动，如走路，正如这位参与者所指出的：

> “(When) you don't feel like you're at the proper weight it makes things difficult. It makes it harder for you to go up a flight of stairs, walk for long distances, stand for long periods of time.” (Pt 9)  
> “（当）你觉得体重不合适时，事情就会变得困难。这让你更难上楼梯、走很长一段路、长时间站立。”（第9部分）

The participants said that binge eating can result in feeling fatigued afterward. Additionally, the participants were impacted by knowing other family members who struggle with diabetes, indicating concerns or motivations to lose weight to reduce their own risk of diabetes based on their family history as shared by this participant:  
参与者表示，暴食可能导致事后感到疲劳。此外，参与者还受到了解其他患有糖尿病的家庭成员的影响，这些成员根据其家族史表达了减肥的担忧或动机，以降低自身糖尿病风险，正如本参与者所分享的：

> “I am trying to lose weight mainly to reduce my chances of becoming a diabetic by a lot, because diabetes is genetic in my family and I have been a borderline diabetic multiple times.” (Pt 24)  
> “我主要想减肥，以大幅降低成为糖尿病的风险，因为糖尿病在我家族中有遗传性，我多次濒临糖尿病。”（第24部分）

#### Impact on Mental Health 对心理健康的影响

The participants' responses reinforced the significant impact that obesity and binge eating have on their mental health. They described feelings of guilt, failure, and low mood that can accompany and prompt episodes of binge eating as well as the ways in which excess weight contributes to low self-esteem. One participant described how binge eating serves as a coping strategy during episodes of depression while recognizing that these eating episodes perpetuate a negative cycle:  
参与者的反馈强化了肥胖和暴食对心理健康的重大影响。他们描述了内疚、失败感和低落情绪，这些情绪可能伴随并引发暴饮暴食，以及过重如何导致自尊心低落。一位参与者描述了暴食在抑郁发作期间的应对策略，同时认识到这些进食发作会延续一个负面循环：

> “My depression came back, and I guess bingeing is the only thing that I guess helps me get through. But when I get through binge eating, then I'm just even more depressed.” (Pt 10)  
> “我的抑郁又回来了，我想暴饮暴食是唯一能帮我挺过去的方法。但当我熬过暴饮暴食后，我反而更抑郁。”（第10部分）

Indeed this example shows how episodes of binge eating yield strong negative emotions and can have instantaneous impacts on mood.  
事实上，这个例子展示了暴食期如何引发强烈的负面情绪，并可能瞬间影响情绪。

#### Impact on Quality of Life对生活质量的影响

The participants described many ways in which they experienced socio-environmental pressures pertaining to their eating and body image that impacted their quality of life, defined as the ability to participate in and enjoy life events. They described having a negative body image and making or experiencing judgments about their food choices. For example, this participant decided to celebrate a “happy moment” with food, and because he judged the amount and type of food he was eating, he hid the experience from his daughter:  
参与者描述了他们在饮食和身体形象方面经历的社会环境压力的多种方式，这些压力影响了他们生活质量，即参与和享受生活事件的能力。他们描述了自己有负面的身体形象，并对自己的饮食选择做出或经历过评判。例如，这位参与者决定用食物庆祝“快乐时刻”，因为他判断自己吃的食物的数量和类型，所以对女儿隐瞒了这段经历：

> “I ate in the car to avoid my daughter seeing me eat this food that I tell her to stay away from. (…) I would LOVE to have been able to celebrate that moment WITH my daughter, with perhaps a better meal.” (Pt 22)  
> “我在车里吃饭，是为了避免女儿看到我吃我叫她远离的食物。(…)我真希望能和女儿一起庆祝那一刻，也许还有一顿更好的饭菜。”（第22部分）

The participants also described feelings of exclusion due to their weight, such as exclusion from daily activities and from experiences that they perceived people have when their weight is in the normal weight range. This participant noted several ways in which she felt being overweight negatively impacted her life:

> “It (overweight) does undermine your ability to attract men to date. It undermines your ability to get a better job. (…) You may even not be able to get a job in leadership in your community because people want to have a representation of their company in a positive way, and society views being skinny as a positive.” (Pt 12)

Another participant described struggling to engage in activities because of physical health limitations as a result of their weight:

> “I want to be around long enough to see (my children's) children and live a quality and productive life. I want to be able to participate in sports and activities and not feel bad because I am running out of breath.” (Pt 3)

This participant tried to avoid activities due to his weight:

> “I find myself trying to avoid family pictures or doing too much in public because I'm uncomfortable with the way I look.” (Pt 6)

The participants described difficulties with how clothes fit as well as the financial burden of binge eating. For this participant, these two factors were related:

> “Binge eating impacts my budget as I'm constantly purchasing the wrong foods and more clothes to fit my growing body.” (Pt 3)

Taken together, the ways in which binge eating and obesity impact people's daily lives provide insights into the types of reasons people are motivated to change their behaviors. They also inform specific domains in which individuals may experience challenges to initiate or sustain behavior change, such as concerns with body image, avoiding people or activities, and finances. Understanding these impacts is beneficial for informing areas to leverage or build added support in interventions.

#### The Snacker 零食客

“ *When I try to watch what I eat, then I get really, really hungry*.” The Snacker feels like they are constantly eating. Their day consists of eating between meals. They are trying to limit their calories because they are concerned about their body size, so they eat a small breakfast and lunch. Throughout the day, though, they get hungry for more food. They get bored sitting at their desk all day, and they find it hard to resist the temptation to buy something from the vending machines. Office birthday parties and donuts at meetings also make it hard for them to stay on track with their plans to lose weight. They like joining their coworkers for happy hour, and they share appetizers and drink beers. Because they ate earlier, they do not eat much for dinner once they get home but tend to snack again while relaxing and watching TV. They are motivated to change their behavior by creating structure to their eating, reducing their desire for snacks, and learning to resist available food. However, they struggle with having constant cravings, never feeling particularly hungry or full, feeling unsatisfied after meals, and eating when they are bored. They are aware that these factors impact their progress.  
“ *当我试图注意吃什么时，我会非常非常饿* 。”零食者感觉自己一直在吃东西。他们的一天包括餐间用餐。他们试图限制热量摄入，因为担心自己的体型，所以早餐和午餐都吃得很简单。不过整天它们会饿，渴望更多食物。他们整天坐在办公桌前感到无聊，很难抗拒去自动售货机买东西的诱惑。办公室生日派对和会议上的甜甜圈也让他们难以坚持减肥计划。他们喜欢和同事一起参加欢乐时光，分享开胃菜和啤酒。因为他们吃得早，回家后晚餐吃得不多，但往往会在放松和看电视时又会吃零食。他们通过为饮食建立结构、减少零食欲望以及学会抵制现有食物来改变行为。然而，他们会不断有渴望，从未感到特别饥饿或饱足，饭后感到不满足，且在无聊时进食。他们意识到这些因素会影响他们的进步。

#### The On-The-Goer 去路人

“ *I'll do good all day, I'll eat light, and then binge at the end of the night, because I'm so famished*.” The On-The-Goer barely has time to eat throughout the day and then eats too much at night. They bustle between responsibilities all day. They work full time and often do not have time in the morning to pack a lunch. They rely on granola bars or leftover snacks to get them through the day. After work, they bring their kids to soccer practice or doctors' appointments and then have to make the kids' dinner before their bedtime routines. It is not until they have a moment to themselves that they eventually realize that they have not eaten much at all and feel famished. It is hard for them not to binge in these moments. These binges remind them that they need to change their eating routine, but they feel overwhelmed by the idea of adding another thing to their list of responsibilities. They are motivated to change their behavior in terms of planning meals in advance, structuring their eating, and learning to provide for their family while caring for themselves. However, they recognize that progress will be challenged by their pattern of going too long without eating, not having enough time for meal planning, feeling exhausted and helpless to make a change, and putting themselves and their health after other responsibilities.  
“ *我整天都乖，吃得清淡，晚上就暴饮暴食，因为我太饿* 了。”“走路者”白天几乎没时间吃东西，晚上又吃得太多。他们整天忙于各种职责之间。他们全职工作，早上常常没时间准备午餐。他们依赖格兰诺拉麦片棒或剩余零食来度过一天。下班后，他们会带孩子去足球训练或看医生，然后在孩子们睡觉前做晚饭。直到他们有片刻属于自己的时间，才意识到自己几乎没吃东西，感到饥饿。他们很难在这些时刻不暴饮暴食。这些暴食提醒他们需要改变饮食习惯，但又被增加一项责任的想法压得喘不过气。他们有动力改变行为，比如提前计划餐食、安排饮食，以及在照顾自己的同时养家糊口。然而，他们也意识到进步会受到长期不进食、缺乏足够时间规划餐食、感到疲惫无力改变以及将自己和健康置于其他责任之后的模式所挑战。

#### The Emotional Eater 情感吞噬者

“ *It's just a constant downhill spiral. And so, when I get depressed, I eat more, and that causes me to get more depressed, and it just doesn't seem to end*.” The Emotional Eater turns to food when they feel stressed or down. When they get overwhelmed about an assignment, they eat to calm down. Unfortunately, this makes them more delayed in starting their assignment, which causes more stress. Because money is tight, they do not like to stock up on extra groceries, but their roommates always share their leftovers and leave snacks which are hard to resist on the counter. They struggle with depression and anxiety; while the initial comfort of eating creates a bit of relief, they feel ashamed and guilty after a binge. They do not want people to know that this is their pattern, so they try to hide the evidence of their binges. They want to change their behavior to no longer manage sadness and emotions with food and to improve their mental health and binge eating, yet currently, food is their coping tool, and they fall into a cycle of eating for comfort and then withdrawing.  
“ *这只是一个持续的下坡螺旋。所以，当我抑郁时，我会吃得更多，这又让我更抑郁，而且似乎永无止* 境。”情绪吞噬者在感到压力或情绪低落时会转向食物。当他们因作业感到压力大时，会吃东西来平复情绪。不幸的是，这会让他们开始作业的时间更晚，进而带来更多压力。由于经济拮据，他们不喜欢囤积额外的杂货，但室友们总是分享剩菜，并在柜台上放一些难以抗拒的零食。他们与抑郁和焦虑作斗争;虽然最初的饮食带来些许安慰，但暴饮暴食后他们会感到羞愧和内疚。他们不想让别人知道这是他们的惯常模式，所以试图掩盖暴食的证据。他们希望改变行为，不再用食物来管理悲伤和情绪，改善心理健康和暴食症，但目前食物成了他们的应对工具，他们陷入了一个为了安慰而吃东西，然后又退缩的循环。

#### The Enthuisiast 热情者

“ *It's really difficult and discouraging because I'm putting so much work into physical exercise, and I just throw it away with binge eating for a few minutes of enjoyment*.” The Enthuisiast is always looking to make changes to their eating. They are very aware of and unhappy about their weight and binge eating. They spend a lot of time researching on different solutions so they can learn about new trends to lose weight. They love to read weight loss blogs and follow influencers' recipes and tips on social media. They are good at creating weight loss plans, but they cannot seem to follow through on their plans and achieve results. The Enthuisiast is very motivated to change their behavior. They want to see “visible success” (e.g., scale numbers decreasing, clothes fitting differently, having a flatter stomach) and to set a clear plan with validation/motivation as they reach their goals. Unfortunately, they find that, although they can organize a plan, they cannot stick with it, and they feel frustrated that they put in effort that is not rewarded with success. They often fail at their goals, like cutting out foods, by indulging in them later or realizing that they set unrealistic exercise goals.  
“ *这真的很难，也很沮丧，因为我花了太多精力锻炼身体，结果* 却为了享受几分钟的乐趣而暴饮暴食。”热情者总是在寻找改变他们的饮食方式。他们非常清楚自己的体重和暴食症。他们花大量时间研究不同的解决方案，以便了解减肥的新趋势。他们喜欢阅读减肥博客，关注社交媒体上网红的食谱和技巧。他们擅长制定减肥计划，但似乎无法坚持执行计划并取得成果。热情者非常有动力改变自己的行为。他们希望看到“明显的成功”（例如，体重秤数字减少、衣服尺寸不同、腹部更平坦），并在实现目标时制定明确的计划和认可和动力。不幸的是，他们发现虽然能组织计划，但无法坚持下去，并且因为付出努力却没有成功而感到沮丧。他们常常因为后来放纵自己去吃，或者意识到自己设定的锻炼目标不切实际，从而未能实现目标，比如戒掉食物。

#### The Planner 策划者

“ *I kind of plan my meals and life around when I can binge eat next time*.” The Planner expects that binge eating is going to happen, so instead of trying to stop it, they plan for it. They mainly binge on holidays and special occasions. They fast and exercise a lot in preparation. Before they binge, they stock up on their favorite binge foods but often end up buying too much. Once they binge, they find that it is hard to get back on track—it is like, “oh well” —and they eat poorly for several more days. Their weight constantly fluctuates because they cycle between denying and indulging on food. Their family history of health problems like diabetes makes them worried about the impact of these weight fluctuations on their overall health. Because of this, the Planner wants to change their behavior and is motivated by the idea of being able to enjoy events without bingeing and eating small portions of celebratory foods without a binge episode, yet they feel that these changes are an uphill battle because they feel like binge episodes are inevitable. They tell themselves that they will work off the calories later but feel nervous about their weight because their binge eating happens more and more often.  
“ *我大概是根据下次能暴饮暴食的时间来安排我的饮食和生活* 。”计划者预料暴饮暴食会发生，所以他们没有试图阻止它，而是提前做好计划。他们主要在节日和特殊场合狂看。他们会禁食并大量锻炼以备战。暴食前，他们会囤积自己喜欢的暴食食物，但往往买得太多。一旦暴饮暴食，他们发现很难重新回到正轨——就像“算了吧”——而且接下来几天饮食都很糟糕。他们的体重不断波动，因为他们在拒绝和放纵食物之间反复波动。他们家族有糖尿病等健康问题史，这让他们担心体重波动对整体健康的影响。因此，计划者希望改变自己的行为，并被能够享受活动而不暴食、吃少量庆祝食物而不暴食的想法所激励，但他们觉得这些改变是一场艰难的战斗，因为他们觉得暴食发作是不可避免的。他们告诉自己以后会消耗这些热量，但因为暴食越来越频繁，对体重感到紧张。

## Discussion 讨论

Understanding the day-to-day experiences of individuals with binge eating and obesity helps inform intervention designs that can be relevant to diverse experiences. By conducting a qualitative needs assessment in real-world contexts, we gained an in-depth understanding of internal and external precipitants as well as consequences associated with obesity and binge eating. In turn, these findings informed the creation of five personas, archetypes of hypothetical users, which, by clarifying the different motivations and challenges behind the experiences of this population, provide a meaningful, tangible foundation to inform intervention design.  
了解暴食和肥胖个体的日常经历，有助于制定适用于多样化经历的干预设计。通过在现实环境中进行定性需求评估，我们深入理解了内在和外部诱因以及肥胖和暴食相关的后果。这些发现反过来促成了五个角色的创建，这些角色是假设用户的原型，通过澄清该群体经历背后的不同动机和挑战，为干预设计提供了有意义且具体的基础。

The findings from our digital diary study showed that individuals have varying precipitants of problematic eating and varying ways in which these health problems impact their lives, consistent with existing literature on these health problems \[e.g., ([^7], [^17])\]. Importantly, however, our qualitative data were captured in the contexts of individuals' everyday lives. Methods like ecological momentary assessment have been used to capture self-reported in-context experiences of people with binge eating ([^38]) but, to our knowledge, have been limited to quantitative data. Collecting in-context qualitative data allowed for an in-depth, narrative understanding of the ways binge eating and obesity manifest throughout the day, including the ways that these conditions are experienced cognitively and emotionally and how they are managed. Thus, our method of using a digital diary study offers another approach that clinical scientists can use to engage with end-users around understanding individual variations in precipitants, outcomes, and motivations.  
我们的数字日记研究结果显示，个体饮食问题的诱因各不相同，这些健康问题对生活的影响方式也各不相同，这与现有相关健康问题文献一致\[例如，（ [^7] ， [^17] ）\]。然而，重要的是，我们的定性数据是在个体日常生活的背景下捕捉的。生态瞬时评估等方法已被用来捕捉暴食者自我报告的情境体验（ [^38] ），但据我们所知，这些数据仅限于定量数据。收集有上下文的定性数据，使得对暴食和肥胖在一天中的表现方式进行了深入的叙述理解，包括这些状况在认知和情感上的体验以及如何管理。因此，我们使用数字日记研究的方法，为临床科学家提供了另一种方法，帮助最终用户理解个体在沉降因素、结局和动机上的差异。

Because individual variation exists, the challenge in creating a digital intervention is making a tool that applies to a broad catchment of users while being relevant to each user's individual needs and contexts. To help address this challenge, we created five personas that represent hypothetical users of our intervention. Based on the findings of our digital diary study, the personas demonstrate different motivations and barriers to behavior change that individuals experience in managing binge eating and weight. The use of personas is helpful for informing how a technology can be designed to meet the diverse needs of individuals. They can reduce self-referential design decisions (e.g., assuming that the user would have the same experiences and preferences as the designer) and help the designer maintain a focus on the specifics of the user's experience instead of the limitations and benefits of a particular technology ([^21], [^39]). They can also be used to represent and communicate the user's profile and needs to various stakeholders, including to users themselves who may prefer to reference a persona's hypothetical experience using a tool than to personalize their feedback \[e.g., ([^40])\]. Similarly, we have developed personas that represent profiles of users with obesity and binge eating and that can thereby help us design an intervention that is ultimately responsive to the specific and varied experiences of users with this health profile.  
由于存在个体差异，创建数字干预的挑战在于打造一个既适用于广泛用户群体，又能满足每个用户个体需求和情境的工具。为了应对这一挑战，我们创建了五个角色扮演，分别代表我们干预的假设使用者。根据我们的数字日记研究发现，这些角色展示了个体在管理暴食和体重时所经历的不同动机和行为改变障碍。角色的使用有助于指导技术如何设计以满足多样化个体的需求。它们可以减少自我指涉的设计决策（例如假设用户会拥有与设计师相同的体验和偏好），并帮助设计师专注于用户体验的具体细节，而非特定技术的局限性和优势（ [^21] ， [^39] ）。它们还可以用来向各类利益相关者表示和传达用户的个人资料和需求，包括那些可能更愿意用工具参考某人角色假设体验而非个性化反馈的用户\[例如，（ [^40] ）\]。同样，我们开发了代表肥胖和暴食用户档案的人物画像，从而帮助我们设计出最终能响应该健康特征用户具体且多样化体验的干预方案。

By capturing and distilling several aspects of the user experience, these personas can help us prioritize the requirements for the intervention product. For example, our personas capture a number of internal and situational factors that influence binge eating, and that must be accounted for in the design process. Whereas the “On-The-Goer” illustrates important situational factors that can precipitate binge eating, like time of day (e.g., propensities toward late-night binge eating) and busyness (e.g., failure to eat throughout the day can exacerbate binge eating), the “Emotional Eater” captures the ways that internal factors precipitate binge eating, such as stress or feeling down. Referencing these personas can allow the designers to keep these user experiences in mind as they prioritize design features. For instance, an intervention is likely to be successful if it not only provides the user with on-demand access to tools based on their perceived need (e.g., as might help the “Emotional Eater” in times of distress) but also pushes reminders and support to users when they most need it (e.g., which could help the “On-The-Goer” take time to eat). We have incorporated such features into our intervention in the form of push notifications to praise or encourage progress toward goals based on users' inputs into the app, along with delivering human coaching; these features enable providing tailored support to individual users' needs.  
通过捕捉和提炼用户体验的多个方面，这些角色扮演可以帮助我们优先排序干预产品的相关需求。例如，我们的人格捕捉了许多影响暴食的内部和情境因素，这些因素必须在设计过程中加以考虑。“在行者”展示了可能引发暴食的重要情境因素，如一天中的时间（例如深夜暴饮暴食倾向）和忙碌程度（例如全天不进食会加重暴食），而“情绪性食客”则反映了内在因素如何促成暴食，如压力或情绪低落。引用这些角色可以让设计师在优先考虑设计特征时，考虑这些用户体验。例如，干预不仅根据用户感知需求提供按需使用工具（例如，在“情绪吞噬者”困境时可能提供帮助），还能在用户最需要时提醒和支持（例如，帮助“进食者”抽时间进食），那么干预很可能成功。我们将这些功能融入干预，以推送通知的形式，基于用户输入的应用来表扬或鼓励目标的进展，同时提供人工指导;这些功能能够根据每个用户的需求提供量身定制的支持。

Our personas also remind us that interventions may be successful to the extent that they address multiple distinct types of users. For instance, a “Snacker” might need help to disrupt recurrent snacking, such as by finding new ways to deal with boredom and establishing a regular pattern of eating instead. By contrast, an “On-The-Goer” might require help in planning meals or need reminders to keep healthy food on hand for snacks. An “Emotional Eater” might benefit from tools that support healthier strategies for coping with emotions that do not involve food. We have designed our intervention to offer an array of psychoeducational material and tools to support such problem areas, including encouraging boredom-reducing activities, meal planning, and addressing negative emotions. As users engage with the intervention, we can begin to leverage data to inform how to tailor these features to specific user profiles to streamline the user experience as has been pursued in other digital health interventions \[e.g., ([^41])\]. In fact, although the personas presented here are distinct hypothetical users (consistent with the persona design), in practice, actual users may overlap across personas. Intervention designs that flexibly deliver features to address multiple challenge areas users face, as well as changes in users' needs over time, would likely help to reflect this reality and thus have heightened relevance to users.  
我们的角色形象还提醒我们，干预措施在能够覆盖多种不同类型的用户时才可能取得成功。例如，“零食爱好者”可能需要帮助来打断反复的零食摄入，比如寻找新的应对无聊的方法，并建立固定的进食模式。相比之下，“随行者”可能需要帮助来规划餐食，或者需要提醒自己准备健康食物作为零食。“情绪性食客”可能会受益于支持更健康应对情绪、不涉及食物的工具。我们设计干预方案，提供一系列心理教育材料和工具，支持这些问题领域，包括鼓励减无聊活动、膳食规划以及处理负面情绪。随着用户参与干预，我们可以开始利用数据，指导如何针对特定用户配置文件定制这些功能，以简化用户体验，正如其他数字健康干预所追求的那样\[例如，（ [^41] ）\]。事实上，尽管这里展示的人物是不同的假设用户（符合角色设计），但实际上，实际用户可能在不同角色之间有重叠。能够灵活提供功能以应对用户面临的多个挑战领域，以及用户需求随时间变化的干预设计，可能会更好地反映这一现实，从而提升用户的相关性。

As we have shown, these design decisions are grounded in the findings from the early user-centered design activities and can be expressed and preliminarily evaluated by referencing the personas; however, they need to be validated through user testing. The subsequent phases of the user-centered design process, which we are employing in this program of research, are to design and iteratively refine prototypes of the intervention in collaboration with end-users and, after the intervention is developed, test it in practice. Taking these steps will be critical for learning whether our design decisions, such as those described above, lead to a positive user experience and sustained engagement in the intervention. The “final” phase of validating the intervention through user testing in practice also affords the opportunity to learn from users ways to optimize the intervention over time, which thus continues the user-centered design process.  
正如我们所展示的，这些设计决策基于早期以用户为中心的设计活动发现，可以通过引用角色来表达和初步评估;不过，它们需要通过用户测试来验证。我们在本研究项目中采用的用户中心设计流程的后续阶段，将与终端用户合作设计和迭代完善干预的原型，并在干预开发完成后进行实际测试。采取这些步骤对于了解我们设计决策（如上述所述）是否能带来积极的用户体验和持续参与度至关重要。通过用户测试验证干预的“最终”阶段，也为用户提供了学习如何随着时间优化干预的机会，从而延续以用户为中心的设计过程。

The strength of this study was applying user-centered design methods to engage with a diverse group of users to delve deeply into their daily experiences and inform the design of our mobile intervention. Moreover, the use of the digital diary study *via* the dscout platform enabled capturing rich qualitative insights in context with relatively low burden on both participants and researchers. However, limitations should be noted. First, the participants were limited to dscout users. Although a core premise of the user-centered design methodology is that the voices of a few individuals are assumed to speak for the broader population ([^42]), the participants in this study may not reflect all individuals with obesity and binge eating who are interested in losing weight and reducing binge eating. We also acknowledge that this is a small sample size in the clinical science field, but it is consistent with sample sizes in design research in the field of human–computer interaction ([^33], [^34]). Second, we used self-reported data to identify eligible participants as having obesity and recurrent binge eating (i.e., using self-reported height, weight, and episodes of binge eating); however, these measurements were not confirmed *via* objective assessment, which can vary from self-report ([^19]). Finally, inter-rater reliability between coders was not assessed, although we note that this measure is not consistently calculated in papers of qualitative data from the field of human–computer interaction ([^43]).  
本研究的优势在于运用以用户为中心的设计方法，与多元化的用户群体互动，深入挖掘他们的日常体验，并为我们的移动干预设计提供参考。此外， *通过* dscout 平台使用数字日记研究，使得能够在相对较低的参与者和研究人员负担较低的背景下捕捉丰富的定性洞见。不过，也需要注意其局限性。首先，参与者仅限于侦察用户。尽管以用户为中心设计方法的核心前提是假设少数人的声音代表更广泛的人群（ [^42] ），但本研究的参与者可能并不代表所有有肥胖和暴食倾向、希望减肥和减少暴食的个体。我们也承认，这在临床科学领域样本量较小，但与人机交互设计研究中的样本量是一致的（ [^33] ， [^34] ）。其次，我们使用自我报告数据识别符合条件的参与者是否存在肥胖和反复暴食（即使用自我报告的身高、体重和暴食发作）;然而，这些测量并未 *通过* 客观评估得到确认，而客观评估可能与自我报告有所不同（ [^19] ）。最后，未评估编码者间的评分者间可靠性，尽管我们注意到，在人机交互领域的定性数据论文中，这一指标的计算并不一致（ [^43] ）。

In conclusion, this study applied user-centered design activities to understand the day-to-day experiences of individuals with binge eating and obesity. These efforts are beneficial for informing intervention designs that can be relevant to diverse experiences and account for variations in these factors. Our process of taking a user-centered design approach to designing a mobile intervention contrasts the typical process that clinical scientists have used to design digital health interventions, that is, typical designs may be based on clinical expertise but do not sufficiently account for the experiences and preferences of the intervention consumer. Accordingly, this paper helps extend the clinical science literature by demonstrating design methods that can help clinical scientists design interventions that meet the needs of and intend to successfully engage its intended consumers. Indeed because personas have not been created to represent people with both obesity and binge eating, our personas and the methods that we employed to create them are useful for informing the design of our own mobile intervention as well as the design of other behavioral interventions. As we have described, the insights from our digital diary study needs assessment and the resulting personas have been used to design prototypes and to confirm that the prototype iterations and the resulting mobile intervention being designed meet the needs, goals, and preferences of end-users.  
总之，本研究运用以用户为中心的设计活动来理解暴食和肥胖个体的日常体验。这些努力有助于制定能够针对多元经验并考虑这些因素差异的干预设计。我们采用以用户为中心的设计方法设计移动干预，这与临床科学家设计数字健康干预的典型流程形成对比，即典型设计可能基于临床专业知识，但未能充分考虑干预使用者的体验和偏好。因此，本文通过展示设计方法，帮助临床科学家设计能够满足并有意成功吸引目标消费者需求的干预措施，从而扩展临床科学文献。事实上，由于角色尚未被创建来代表肥胖和暴食者，我们的角色及其创建方法对于指导我们自身的移动干预以及其他行为干预的设计非常有用。正如我们所描述的，数字日记研究需求评估及其所得人物角色的洞见已被用来设计原型，并确认原型迭代及所设计的移动干预是否满足终端用户的需求、目标和偏好。

#### The Snacker 零食客

“ *When I try to watch what I eat, then I get really, really hungry*.” The Snacker feels like they are constantly eating. Their day consists of eating between meals. They are trying to limit their calories because they are concerned about their body size, so they eat a small breakfast and lunch. Throughout the day, though, they get hungry for more food. They get bored sitting at their desk all day, and they find it hard to resist the temptation to buy something from the vending machines. Office birthday parties and donuts at meetings also make it hard for them to stay on track with their plans to lose weight. They like joining their coworkers for happy hour, and they share appetizers and drink beers. Because they ate earlier, they do not eat much for dinner once they get home but tend to snack again while relaxing and watching TV. They are motivated to change their behavior by creating structure to their eating, reducing their desire for snacks, and learning to resist available food. However, they struggle with having constant cravings, never feeling particularly hungry or full, feeling unsatisfied after meals, and eating when they are bored. They are aware that these factors impact their progress.  
“ *当我试图注意吃什么时，我会非常非常饿* 。”零食者感觉自己一直在吃东西。他们的一天包括餐间用餐。他们试图限制热量摄入，因为担心自己的体型，所以早餐和午餐都吃得很简单。不过整天它们会饿，渴望更多食物。他们整天坐在办公桌前感到无聊，很难抗拒去自动售货机买东西的诱惑。办公室生日派对和会议上的甜甜圈也让他们难以坚持减肥计划。他们喜欢和同事一起参加欢乐时光，分享开胃菜和啤酒。因为他们吃得早，回家后晚餐吃得不多，但往往会在放松和看电视时又会吃零食。他们通过为饮食建立结构、减少零食欲望以及学会抵制现有食物来改变行为。然而，他们会不断有渴望，从未感到特别饥饿或饱足，饭后感到不满足，且在无聊时进食。他们意识到这些因素会影响他们的进步。

#### The On-The-Goer 去路人

“ *I'll do good all day, I'll eat light, and then binge at the end of the night, because I'm so famished*.” The On-The-Goer barely has time to eat throughout the day and then eats too much at night. They bustle between responsibilities all day. They work full time and often do not have time in the morning to pack a lunch. They rely on granola bars or leftover snacks to get them through the day. After work, they bring their kids to soccer practice or doctors' appointments and then have to make the kids' dinner before their bedtime routines. It is not until they have a moment to themselves that they eventually realize that they have not eaten much at all and feel famished. It is hard for them not to binge in these moments. These binges remind them that they need to change their eating routine, but they feel overwhelmed by the idea of adding another thing to their list of responsibilities. They are motivated to change their behavior in terms of planning meals in advance, structuring their eating, and learning to provide for their family while caring for themselves. However, they recognize that progress will be challenged by their pattern of going too long without eating, not having enough time for meal planning, feeling exhausted and helpless to make a change, and putting themselves and their health after other responsibilities.  
“ *我整天都乖，吃得清淡，晚上就暴饮暴食，因为我太饿* 了。”“走路者”白天几乎没时间吃东西，晚上又吃得太多。他们整天忙于各种职责之间。他们全职工作，早上常常没时间准备午餐。他们依赖格兰诺拉麦片棒或剩余零食来度过一天。下班后，他们会带孩子去足球训练或看医生，然后在孩子们睡觉前做晚饭。直到他们有片刻属于自己的时间，才意识到自己几乎没吃东西，感到饥饿。他们很难在这些时刻不暴饮暴食。这些暴食提醒他们需要改变饮食习惯，但又被增加一项责任的想法压得喘不过气。他们有动力改变行为，比如提前计划餐食、安排饮食，以及在照顾自己的同时养家糊口。然而，他们也意识到进步会受到长期不进食、缺乏足够时间规划餐食、感到疲惫无力改变以及将自己和健康置于其他责任之后的模式所挑战。

#### The Emotional Eater 情感吞噬者

“ *It's just a constant downhill spiral. And so, when I get depressed, I eat more, and that causes me to get more depressed, and it just doesn't seem to end*.” The Emotional Eater turns to food when they feel stressed or down. When they get overwhelmed about an assignment, they eat to calm down. Unfortunately, this makes them more delayed in starting their assignment, which causes more stress. Because money is tight, they do not like to stock up on extra groceries, but their roommates always share their leftovers and leave snacks which are hard to resist on the counter. They struggle with depression and anxiety; while the initial comfort of eating creates a bit of relief, they feel ashamed and guilty after a binge. They do not want people to know that this is their pattern, so they try to hide the evidence of their binges. They want to change their behavior to no longer manage sadness and emotions with food and to improve their mental health and binge eating, yet currently, food is their coping tool, and they fall into a cycle of eating for comfort and then withdrawing.  
“ *这只是一个持续的下坡螺旋。所以，当我抑郁时，我会吃得更多，这又让我更抑郁，而且似乎永无止* 境。”情绪吞噬者在感到压力或情绪低落时会转向食物。当他们因作业感到压力大时，会吃东西来平复情绪。不幸的是，这会让他们开始作业的时间更晚，进而带来更多压力。由于经济拮据，他们不喜欢囤积额外的杂货，但室友们总是分享剩菜，并在柜台上放一些难以抗拒的零食。他们与抑郁和焦虑作斗争;虽然最初的饮食带来些许安慰，但暴饮暴食后他们会感到羞愧和内疚。他们不想让别人知道这是他们的惯常模式，所以试图掩盖暴食的证据。他们希望改变行为，不再用食物来管理悲伤和情绪，改善心理健康和暴食症，但目前食物成了他们的应对工具，他们陷入了一个为了安慰而吃东西，然后又退缩的循环。

#### The Enthuisiast 热情者

“ *It's really difficult and discouraging because I'm putting so much work into physical exercise, and I just throw it away with binge eating for a few minutes of enjoyment*.” The Enthuisiast is always looking to make changes to their eating. They are very aware of and unhappy about their weight and binge eating. They spend a lot of time researching on different solutions so they can learn about new trends to lose weight. They love to read weight loss blogs and follow influencers' recipes and tips on social media. They are good at creating weight loss plans, but they cannot seem to follow through on their plans and achieve results. The Enthuisiast is very motivated to change their behavior. They want to see “visible success” (e.g., scale numbers decreasing, clothes fitting differently, having a flatter stomach) and to set a clear plan with validation/motivation as they reach their goals. Unfortunately, they find that, although they can organize a plan, they cannot stick with it, and they feel frustrated that they put in effort that is not rewarded with success. They often fail at their goals, like cutting out foods, by indulging in them later or realizing that they set unrealistic exercise goals.  
“ *这真的很难，也很沮丧，因为我花了太多精力锻炼身体，结果* 却为了享受几分钟的乐趣而暴饮暴食。”热情者总是在寻找改变他们的饮食方式。他们非常清楚自己的体重和暴食症。他们花大量时间研究不同的解决方案，以便了解减肥的新趋势。他们喜欢阅读减肥博客，关注社交媒体上网红的食谱和技巧。他们擅长制定减肥计划，但似乎无法坚持执行计划并取得成果。热情者非常有动力改变自己的行为。他们希望看到“明显的成功”（例如，体重秤数字减少、衣服尺寸不同、腹部更平坦），并在实现目标时制定明确的计划和认可和动力。不幸的是，他们发现虽然能组织计划，但无法坚持下去，并且因为付出努力却没有成功而感到沮丧。他们常常因为后来放纵自己去吃，或者意识到自己设定的锻炼目标不切实际，从而未能实现目标，比如戒掉食物。

#### The Planner 策划者

“ *I kind of plan my meals and life around when I can binge eat next time*.” The Planner expects that binge eating is going to happen, so instead of trying to stop it, they plan for it. They mainly binge on holidays and special occasions. They fast and exercise a lot in preparation. Before they binge, they stock up on their favorite binge foods but often end up buying too much. Once they binge, they find that it is hard to get back on track—it is like, “oh well” —and they eat poorly for several more days. Their weight constantly fluctuates because they cycle between denying and indulging on food. Their family history of health problems like diabetes makes them worried about the impact of these weight fluctuations on their overall health. Because of this, the Planner wants to change their behavior and is motivated by the idea of being able to enjoy events without bingeing and eating small portions of celebratory foods without a binge episode, yet they feel that these changes are an uphill battle because they feel like binge episodes are inevitable. They tell themselves that they will work off the calories later but feel nervous about their weight because their binge eating happens more and more often.
